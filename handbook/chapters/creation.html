Character Creation at a Glance

Create a character in this order:

  1. Generate three seven-result arrays and choose one complete array.
  2. Assign its results to the six ordinary Attributes and Luck. Record a Sanity Score of 18 and Current Sanity 18.
  3. Choose a player race, apply its adjustments and maximums, and choose a prime Attribute when the race grants one.
  1. Decide where the character was raised.
  2. Choose one starting Job at level 1 and apply its Starting Package and level-1 features.
  3. Record the resulting starting languages, then choose starting Skills, any granted feat, and DM-approved possessions.
  4. After worn armor and every other starting choice are known, record the character's final starting values and personal details.

Attributes and Ability Modifiers

Attribute Scores and Modifiers

A character has six ordinary attributes: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma. Each has an Attribute Score and an Ability Modifier.

The full Attribute Score measures the character's raw capacity. When a rule calls for an attribute check, roll d20; a result equal to or lower than the score after bonuses and penalties succeeds. Most other rules use the Ability Modifier shown in the table.

Attribute Score Ability Modifier
5 -3
6-7 -2
8-9 -1
10-11 +0
12-13 +1
14-15 +2
16-17 +3
18-19 +4
20-21 +5
22-23 +6
24 +7
25 +8

A score of 25 receives the modifier that 26 would ordinarily provide. Scores above 25 return to the normal progression: 26-27 is +8, 28-29 is +9, and 30-31 is +10. Such values require an explicit extraordinary source and are not part of ordinary character generation.

Every final ordinary player attribute must be at least 5. Luck follows its separate generation rule and may receive a lower result. 
Racial maximum, including any prime-attribute increase, limits normal permanent advancement. A specific magical or extraordinary effect may explicitly allow you to exceed it.

What the Attributes Represent

Attribute Meaning and principal uses
Strength (STR) Physical power and leverage. It governs ordinary melee attacks and damage, Block, carrying capacity, and many physical maneuvers.
Dexterity (DEX) Agility, coordination, reaction speed, and physical precision. It governs Initiative, ordinary ranged attacks, Dodge, Reflex saves, Ride, and readily accessible equipment.
Constitution (CON) Health, endurance, recovery, and resistance to bodily harm. It determines base Life Points and contributes to Physical Energy, Fortitude, running endurance, wound survival, and recovery.
Intelligence (INT) Learning, reasoning, disciplined concentration, and aptitude for acquiring skills. It governs spell Concentration capacity and Defensive Casting and helps determine additional starting skill choices.
Wisdom (WIS) Judgment, awareness, magical reserve, and force of will. It governs Will saves, the amount of Magical Energy granted by Wisdom, Magical Energy recovery, and certain spell-preservation checks.
Charisma (CHA) Presence, expression, and force of personality. It governs social skills and the default Difficulty Classes of spells and abilities.

These are the attributes' principal uses. A skill, class, spell, talent, or other specific rule may use an attribute in another appropriate way.

Luck and Sanity

Luck. Luck is generated alongside the six ordinary attributes. Assign one of the character's seven generated results to Luck. Luck has no racial modifier, cannot be a prime attribute, and produces no Ability Modifier. Its maximum is 18, or 19 when its generation roll showed four 6s.

Luck measures favorable or unfavorable uncertainty. When the DM calls for a Luck check, roll d20 equal to or below the adjusted Luck score. Bonuses raise the effective threshold and penalties lower it. Luck may determine whether a misplaced item is found, whether a plausible improvised tool is nearby, whether an appropriately trained character can locate a possible environmental resource, or which of several reasonable encounter circumstances occurs. It cannot create something impossible or replace relevant knowledge and training. Failure may establish an unfavorable circumstance.

Luck also provides a small reserve of character-saving rerolls:

Luck score Luck rerolls per story arc
15 or lower 0
16-17 1
18 2
19 3

The DM decides when a new story arc begins and these uses refresh. A story arc may span a substantial period of play; Luck does not refresh each session, day, or encounter.

After seeing the result of one of your own d20 rolls, spend one Luck reroll to roll it again and keep whichever result is more favorable. If Advantage or Disadvantage applied, reroll one chosen die, resolve the revised roll, and keep the more favorable complete result. Before making a disadvantaged roll, you may instead spend one use to cancel the remaining 
Disadvantage and roll one ordinary d20. Damage dice and other non-d20 rolls are not normally eligible, but the DM may permit an exception when the requested intervention is genuinely a matter of luck. Luck has no other universal benefit.

Sanity Score and Current Sanity. Sanity is not generated or assigned and produces no Ability Modifier. Every new character begins with a Sanity Score of 18 and Current Sanity 18.

Sanity Score is the permanent measure of the character's connection to ordinary reality, what they regard as normal, and how much horror or hardship they have absorbed. It sets maximum Current Sanity. Current Sanity is a separate HP-like pool that receives Sanity damage. Ordinary Sanity damage reduces Current Sanity without reducing the permanent score.

Severe experiences can permanently lower Sanity Score, representing both lasting damage and hardening. When this happens, maximum Current Sanity falls with it. If Current Sanity exceeds the new maximum, reduce it to that maximum; otherwise leave it unchanged. A character whose Sanity Score falls from 18 to 14 while at Current Sanity 12 remains at 12. One who was at Current Sanity 16 falls to 14.

Lower Sanity can make a character less vulnerable to familiar bloodshed, death, filth, and hardship, while making them increasingly alienated from ordinary society. High Sanity is therefore not simply better than low Sanity.

  • 18: The ordinary starting point: a broadly normal upbringing without close participation in killing, murder, disease, or extreme conditions.
  • 17-16: Meaningful hardship without losing ordinary social function. Violent bereavement or living through a damaged city may fall here.
  • 15-14: Substantial direct exposure to war, killing, blood, filth, lost comrades, or repulsive acts committed to survive.
  • Around 12: Hardening is visible in habits and outlook. Self-care or possessions may be neglected, tics may emerge, and the character has reconciled themselves to conclusions that more sheltered people would reject.
  • 10 or lower: The character is becoming genuinely dysfunctional in ordinary society, and other people can readily perceive that something is deeply wrong.
  • 8-7: The character normally approaches or crosses the boundary of playability because severe trauma-driven or socially incompatible behavior prevents ordinary social function.

These values are interpretive anchors, not mandatory biographies or automatic behavior packages.

Luck and Sanity · continued

Sanity checks and damage. The DM may call for a Sanity check when an event meaningfully defies the character's understanding of reality or exceeds what their history has normalized. The DM gives the event an implicit qualitative Sanity threshold. If the character's Sanity Score is equal to or below that threshold, the character is sufficiently hardened and makes no check. A lower threshold represents a more extreme event because it can affect more heavily hardened characters.

If a check is required, roll d20. The character must roll higher than their Sanity Score to remain unaffected. Failure inflicts Sanity damage and reduces Current Sanity. This unusual roll deliberately makes high-Sanity characters more sensitive once an event qualifies, while hardened characters are less likely to react to familiar mundane horror.

Sanity damage has no fixed severity table. The DM considers the event, its implicit threshold, the character's Sanity Score and history, the distance between that score and the threshold, and the surrounding circumstances. Mild shock commonly inflicts 1d6 Sanity damage, moderate shock commonly inflicts 1d10, and severe or overwhelming shock may inflict several dice or another appropriate amount.

Recovery. Current Sanity recovers through genuinely calming, grounding, reassuring, or pleasurable experiences appropriate to the character. Gambling, visiting a brothel, resting beside a river, wandering open plains, or watching the sun rise after a horrific night may all help, usually restoring 1d6 Sanity. 
One ordinary night's rest does not automatically refill Current Sanity. Long periods of safe downtime generally restore it fully, while a dungeon expedition, military campaign, or other sustained crisis does not. Abilities and divine effects may also restore Current Sanity, especially when they remove or reframe the cause of the horror. Exact recovery remains contextual and DM-adjudicated. As an example, the DM may assign Xd6 Sanity 'healing' when a character has had a good chance to recover, or full when they've had a good, long break from stressful circumstances/adventuring. 

Sanity Shocked. When Current Sanity reaches 0, the character becomes Sanity Shocked and rolls d20 equal to or below half their Sanity Score.

On success, the character becomes Resolute for the remainder of the encounter or approximately one hour, whichever better fits the situation. Resolute fully restores Current Sanity, grants +4 to appropriate

actions, and restores 1d10 Current Sanity to the character's team each turn when the character acts inspirationally.

On failure, the player or DM selects a fitting reaction: Paranoid, Fearful, Selfish, Hopeless, Irrational, or Abusive. The reaction guides portrayal rather than imposing an absolute script. Whenever the character attempts an action in this state, the DM rolls d100. On 1-49, the character acts normally. On 50-100, the DM determines an appropriate altered action or the character accomplishes nothing useful, perhaps freezing, cackling, mumbling incoherently, or staring in terror.

Sanity Shocked and Recovery

A character who fails to become Resolute remains Sanity Shocked for the stressful scene, as defined by the DM. Continuing danger may extend this across several encounters.

A calm interval—perhaps ten minutes spent settling down—can suspend the reaction and its action restrictions. This does not restore Current Sanity. While Current Sanity remains at 0 or below, the character can act normally until another Sanity check is required or the DM judges renewed stress sufficient to reactivate the effects.

Unhinged. Sanity damage can continue below 0. When accumulated damage reaches twice the Sanity Score—so Current Sanity has reached the negative of that score—the character becomes Unhinged and passes to DM control. An Unhinged character may act self-destructively or irrationally, or may cease meaningful action entirely.

The Jaded feat can protect a sufficiently hardened character from Sanity checks and Fear tests caused by mundane death, ordinary violence, or most monstrous abominations. Exceptional terrors of the Abyss still apply. Its prerequisites, price, and complete procedure appear in Talents and Feats.

Generate and Assign Attributes

Generate Three Arrays

Generate three complete arrays, each containing seven results. Generate every result as follows:

  1. Roll 4d6.
  2. If one or more dice show 1, choose one of those dice and reroll it once. Keep the new result even if it is another 1. Do not reroll any other dice showing 1.
  3. Drop the lowest die and total the other three.
  4. If all four dice show 6, the result is 19 rather than 18.

Repeat this procedure until the array contains seven results, then generate two more complete arrays in the same way.

Choose and Assign One Array

Choose one of the three complete arrays and discard the other two. Results cannot be exchanged or combined between arrays.

Assign the chosen array's seven results freely among Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, and Luck. Sanity is not assigned; it begins at 18.

Apply Racial Modifiers and Maximums

After assigning the array, choose a race from the next section and apply its adjustments. Each racial profile lists the maximum a member of that race can normally reach in each attribute. The same number determines the character's starting adjustment: subtract 20 from the listed maximum.

  • A listed maximum of 22 adds +2 to the assigned result.
  • A listed maximum of 19 applies -1 to the assigned result.
  • A listed maximum of 20 makes no change.

A Human also chooses one prime attribute from Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma. Add +3 to both its starting value and permanent maximum. A Half-Elf chooses one prime attribute in the same way and adds +1 instead. Luck and Sanity cannot be prime attributes.

Apply adjustments in this order: assign the generated result, apply the racial adjustment, then apply any prime-attribute increase. Every resulting attribute must be at least 5. Try another assignment or another generated array if one falls below 5. If none of the three arrays can keep every attribute at 5 or higher, generate three new arrays.

The racial maximum prevents ordinary permanent advancement beyond it. A miracle, wish, or similarly extraordinary effect may explicitly exceed that maximum.

Example: Racial Adjustment and Prime Attribute

A Half-Elf assigns 18 to Dexterity. The Half-Elf Dexterity maximum is 22, so the racial adjustment adds +2. If Dexterity is also chosen as the character's prime attribute, add another +1 to its starting value and maximum. The character begins with Dexterity 21 and has a permanent Dexterity maximum of 23.

Races

Reading a Racial Profile

The six attribute values in a racial profile show the race's ordinary permanent maximums. They also determine its starting adjustments, as explained in Apply Racial Modifiers and Maximums above.

Size determines which size rules apply. Speed is the distance the character can cover with ordinary Movement. The ME multiplier determines how much maximum Magical Energy the character gains from Wisdom. Multiply permanent Wisdom by this number and round down. If permanent Wisdom changes, calculate this amount again, then add any fixed ME bonuses.

Magic Protection (MP) is passive protection that reduces spell damage by its listed amount. It is not Magical Energy and cannot be spent.

Human

Humans are Vanaheim's most adaptable people. Their nations and traditions differ enormously, and Humans can master almost any calling. Their defining strength is the speed with which they learn, specialize, and advance.

  • Choose one prime attribute from STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, or CHA. Add +3 to both its starting value and permanent maximum.
  • Begin with one ordinary feat for which the character qualifies and one additional starting skill selection.
  • Gain +1 XP after every session that awards XP.
  • A Human who begins play as a Mage gains +5 ME. Beginning as an Acolyte does not grant this bonus, and choosing a Mage Job later does not grant it.

Half-Elf

Half-Elves combine Human adaptability with elven longevity. They ordinarily mature near a Human pace until roughly age 35 to 40, after which aging slows dramatically. No one can reliably predict a Half-Elf's lifespan: some live for centuries, some for far longer, and some may be immortal. Near the final portion of life, the skin begins to harden and crack like bark and the hair loses color, though the Half-Elf rarely develops the frail appearance of a very old Human.

  • Choose one prime attribute from STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, or CHA. Add +1 to both its starting value and permanent maximum.
  • Gain Low-light Vision.

Low-light Vision: In dim light, double the distance at which the Half-Elf can see, to a maximum of 200 feet and never farther than the same scene could be seen in daylight. Low-light Vision provides no sight in total darkness.

Elf

The shared Elf profile covers Sylvar, Pyran, and common elves. Sylvar are the oldest and most otherworldly elven people; Pyran are protective forest-dwellers shaped by long contact with humanity; common elves descend from Pyran communities that lived among Humans for generations. Elves are immortal in lifespan but can be killed. They retain a young-adult appearance rather than becoming visibly elderly, and chronological age need not match apparent age.

  • An Elf player character must have a Good alignment.
  • Gain Sneak, Low-light Vision, racial Detect Magic, Elven Reverie and Sleep Immunity, Mana Well, and Cold-Iron Weakness.
  • An ordinary Elf has Speed 30 feet. A Pyran Elf instead has Speed 35 feet. Unless another rule says otherwise, Sylvar, Pyran, and common Elves share the same racial traits.

Sneak (Talent): An Elf with Sneak automatically succeeds on Stealth checks against creatures that are not actively on guard, provided the Elf remains outside their immediate line of sight. If the Elf also possesses the Hide Talent, Hide grants +10 on Stealth while perfectly still in a place where hiding is physically possible; Sneak allows the Elf to move while retaining that bonus so long as concealment still permits Stealth. Detect Invisibility reveals an Elf using Hide.

Low-light Vision: In dim light, double the distance at which the Elf can see, to a maximum of 200 feet and never farther than the same scene could be seen in daylight. Low-light Vision provides no sight in total darkness.

Racial Detect Magic: The Elf can invoke the Detect Magic spell by concentrating. Invoking it costs the same ME and uses the same Spell Speed, concentration rules, and effect as the spell, but requires no verbal, somatic, or material components.

Elven Reverie and Sleep Immunity: Elves are immune to mundane and magical sleep. Instead of sleeping, an Elf spends four hours in quiet Elven Reverie, recalling memories without dreaming. Reverie satisfies the need for sleep and refreshes Mana Well; it does not prevent Fatigue, Exhaustion, or Unconsciousness from another cause. Recovering ME, HP, PE, and physical fatigue still requires the full rest period specified by those rules; the four hours of Reverie may occur within it.

Mana Well: Every Sylvar, Pyran, and common Elf represented by this profile possesses Mana Well from level 1, though it first becomes usable when one of the Elf's Jobs reaches level 10. Dark Elves are safe recipients but do not possess Mana Well. Use the Elf's highest Job level for this ability; if several Jobs are tied, use the one acquired first.

  • The Elf gains one use at level 10, a second at level 20, and another every ten levels thereafter. Activating a use is a Main Action.
  • Each use restores ME equal to half that Job level, rounded down, up to the recipient's maximum. At level 10 it restores 5 ME once; at level 14 it restores 7 ME once; at level 20 it restores 10 ME and can be used twice.
  • The Elf may restore their own ME or touch another creature to transfer the same amount. Completing four hours of Elven Reverie restores all spent uses, no more than once per 24 hours.
  • Elves, including Dark Elves, and Sprites / Fae can receive this transfer safely. Every other race makes a Will save when receiving it: DC 15 for the first transfer, increasing permanently by 1 with every later transfer to that recipient, regardless of the donor or the result of earlier saves. Failure causes addiction to Elves and their Mana Well energy; the DM adjudicates the consequences.

Cold-Iron Weakness: Elves are vulnerable to cold iron. After applicable protection reduces a cold-iron attack's damage, add half the remaining damage, rounding down, before Physical Energy mitigation. If protection reduces the damage to 0, the weakness creates no damage.

Dwarf

Dwarves are broad-built, long-lived people renowned for metalwork, stonecraft, and engineering. Wars with the Dark Elves shattered most of their ancient underground realms, leaving relatively few free communities on the surface or beneath it; Vaelrain remains one of the surviving free underground settlements. Dwarves commonly live for approximately 300 to 400 years.

  • A Dwarf is Medium and has Speed 30 feet.
  • Gain Infravision.

Infravision: The Dwarf perceives heat patterns within 60 feet, including in darkness. Infravision does not reveal cold detail that produces no meaningful temperature contrast.

Sprite

Sprites are ageless magical beings whose maximum ME is also their life force. Outside a place with sufficiently abundant ambient magic, each Sprite people loses maximum ME at the rate stated by its profile. A Sprite dies if its maximum ME reaches 0; its animating being and soul are gone rather than merely resting or unconscious.

Low-light Vision: In dim light, double the distance at which the Sprite can see, to a maximum of 200 feet and never farther than the same scene could be seen in daylight. Low-light Vision provides no sight in total darkness.

Racial Detect Magic: The Sprite can invoke the Detect Magic spell by concentrating. Invoking it costs the same ME and uses the same Spell Speed, concentration rules, and effect as the spell, but requires no verbal, somatic, or material components.

Cold-Iron Weakness: Sprites are vulnerable to cold iron. After applicable protection reduces a cold-iron attack's damage, add half the remaining damage, rounding down, before Physical Energy mitigation. If protection reduces the damage to 0, the weakness creates no damage.

Sand runners: Sand runners remain NPC-only in the current core rules. They are Medium, human-sized Sprites with subtly different eyes, slightly sharper teeth, and wings that fold beneath clothing. A Sand runner outside sufficiently abundant magic loses 1 maximum ME per month. Returning to abundant magic allows that loss to recover, but the creature profile or DM determines the rate. Use the Sand runner's creature profile for its land Speed, flying Speed, and other abilities.

Giant

Giants retain their Large build and Speed 30 feet. A Giant's creature profile lists any exceptional abilities it possesses, such as Bash, Enhanced Damage, or Boulder.

Choose a Starting Job

Choose One Starting Job

Choose one Job from Classes whose entry requirements the character satisfies. The character begins at Job level 1 and Overall Level 1 with no unspent XP. Restricted Jobs require the permission stated in their profiles.

Apply everything explicitly granted by the Job's Starting Package and its other level-1 features. A rank or talent-access list shows what the character may learn or purchase. Entries on that list are not gained automatically unless the Job says otherwise.

If the starting Job belongs to the Acolyte family, choose its permanent HP, ME, or balanced progression now. If a Human's starting Job belongs to the Mage family, add the Human's +5 ME after applying the ordinary Mage level-1 ME gain.

Starting Skills and Languages

Every character begins fluent in one regional language associated with where the character was raised. The regional languages used for character creation are Ayamaoan, Taslamaran, Uxmaln, Zhenshi, Aghelian, and Imperial. Vanaheim has no universal Common language.

  • An Elf also begins fluent in Sylvan.
  • A Dwarf also begins fluent in Deep Tongue.
  • A Sprite also begins fluent in Sylvan, though Sprites remain NPC-only in the current core rules.
  • A character whose starting Job belongs to the Mage family also begins fluent in Arcane.

These granted languages are trained Knowledge (Language: specific language) fields and use no starting Skill choices. Every additional language is learned as a separate Knowledge (Language: specific language) Skill and uses one starting Skill choice unless another rule grants it. Learning a special language during creation requires DM permission and appropriate access.

After recording the granted languages, choose starting Skills in this order:

  1. Choose three Skills appropriate to the training of the starting Job, subject to DM approval.
  2. Add unrestricted Skill choices equal to the character's positive permanent Intelligence modifier after racial and prime-Attribute adjustments. A modifier of +0 or lower adds no choices and never removes any of the three Job-based choices.
  3. A Human adds one further unrestricted Skill choice.

Skills cannot be purchased twice. Every separately named Craft, Knowledge, Performance, or Profession field is a different Skill. These choices grant trained Skills, not Skill Masteries, and acquiring another Job later does not re-grant them. A character may spend an eligible choice on an additional language as described above.

Starting Talents and Equipment

Record every automatic racial and Job feature. A Human also chooses one ordinary feat for which the character qualifies. Complete feat and Talent choices use Talents and Feats; access to a Talent is permission to learn it, not an automatic grant.

The DM or the adventure supplies or approves starting possessions. Equipment Training permits the character to use the named equipment. Character Creation supplies no universal starting wealth or item package.

Choose worn armor before calculating final maximum HP because Armor HP increases the wearer's one HP pool while the armor is worn.
