Talents and Feats

Talents and Feats are exceptional abilities learned beyond ordinary Skills. Ordinary Talents are tied to Jobs and their rank-access tables. Feats are learned separately from Job progression. Grandmaster Talents are the character-wide exceptional advancement available after the level-31 Grand Master gate and use their own procedure below.

Talents

Learning Talents

A Talent is an ability made available by a Job's rank-access table or by another explicit grant. Reaching a listed rank permits the character to buy the Talent; it does not grant every Talent at that rank automatically.

A Talent is learned once. Access to the same Talent through another Job does not grant a second copy, and it does not refund XP already spent. A Talent creates numbered tiers or another exception only when its own entry explicitly says so.

When a character's Jobs offer the same Talent at different ranks, use the earliest rank the character has already reached. This determines both access and XP price; the character's Main Job does not matter.

Talents available through Expert rank may be self-trained when the character has the necessary time and opportunity. Talents available at Mentor rank or later require a willing instructor who knows the Talent or is otherwise established as capable of teaching it. There is no single training time that applies to every Talent.

Individual Talent entries may impose additional prerequisites or restrictions. Job access and XP payment do not waive them. An Exclusive Talent is available only through the named Job unless another rule explicitly grants access.

Some Starting Packages or other rules grant a named Talent automatically. An automatic grant costs no XP and does not become an additional copy if the character later finds purchase access through another Job.

Grandmaster Talents

Grandmaster Talents, abbreviated GM Talents, do not use the ordinary Job-rank price table. A character becomes eligible after one Job reaches level 31 and the DM recognizes the character as a Grand Master.

Record every GM Talent on one character-wide known list. A new GM Talent costs 10 XP for each GM Talent the character will know after learning it: 10 XP for the first, 20 XP for the second, 30 XP for the third, and so on. Every numbered entry is purchased and counted separately. There is no fixed maximum, shared tier sequence, capacity track, or separate Grandmaster level.

Each entry states GM Talent Requirement: N, meaning the minimum number of other GM Talents the character must already know. It also states its own prerequisites and training or achievement gate. GM Talents are permanent and are not ordinarily retrained, exchanged, or refunded.

The complete current engine and initial nineteen-entry catalogue belong to the separate GM and Beyond expansion. The universal session-XP procedure remains DM-facing and is not repeated here.

Reading a Talent Entry

Every Talent entry begins with a classification label:

  • TALENT identifies a Talent whether it is purchased or granted automatically.
  • MASTERY appears beside TALENT only when the ability is an explicit member of a Class Mastery. It does not mean Master rank or Skill Mastery.
  • JOB FEATURE identifies an automatic Job feature included here for discovery. It is not a purchasable Talent merely because it appears in this chapter.

An entry then gives the Jobs that provide access, its XP price or automatic-grant status, any additional prerequisites, and its effect.

Job-defining abilities are reproduced here from Classes. Bard entries give each ability's own rules and link to the shared performance rules in Classes. Teaching examples remain in Classes.

Alertness

TALENT
Access: Barbarian
XP: 10 XP

Alertness gives the Barbarian a supernatural awareness of danger. The Barbarian cannot be Surprised or made Flat-Footed unless the attacker is a Rogue whose Rogue Job level is at least 5 higher than the Barbarian's Job level, or a Scout whose Scout Job level is at least 5 higher and who uses Ambush.

When a concealed, potentially dangerous supernatural presence or effect is within 60 feet, the DM secretly makes a Wisdom check for the Barbarian. Success reveals that something is wrong, but not its identity, nature, or location.

Invisible creatures, unseen magical forces, unnatural conditions, curses, hauntings, and magical traps may trigger this sense. Ordinary mundane traps and harmless or beneficial magic do not. The DM determines when the hidden check is made.

Ambush

TALENT · MASTERY
Access: Scout
XP: 15 XP

Ranged Mastery, Rapid Fire, and Ambush form one package. They may work together when their individual requirements are met. Selecting another Job's Class Mastery suppresses every Scout-Mastery benefit for that round. If Ranged Mastery or Ambush first becomes relevant through an opening attack, lock or choose Scout Mastery immediately before that Attack roll. Ambush can therefore select Scout Mastery during Round 0. Rapid Fire cannot operate during Round 0 because it requires a Full Attack.

Ambush. Ambush is a purchased Master talent that may be used only on the Scout's first attack attempt of a combat. Before rolling, the Scout may declare it for a ranged attack against a target within 60 feet that cannot perceive or anticipate the Scout. Add the Scout Job level to that Attack roll. The bonus applies only to that attack and can increase damage only through positive Attack Margin. It does not apply to a Rapid Fire attack or another projectile. The opportunity is expended whether the attack hits or misses.

Scout Camouflage can help establish Ambush but is not required when the Scout is otherwise genuinely unperceived and unanticipated. Ambush may initiate combat during Round 0 because it is a Scout Job Mastery. The Round 0 attack cannot receive the Adventurer Chassis's once-per-battle damage addition. An otherwise qualifying Ambush in Round 1 or later may receive that separate addition on its resulting damage roll.

Arcane Ward

TALENT
Access: Sorcerer
XP: 15 XP

Using Arcane Ward takes a Main Action. The Sorcerer creates a visible, stationary arcane glyph filling the Sorcerer's current space and hovering just above the supporting surface. It lasts for 1 minute.

Only the Sorcerer who created the Ward receives its benefits, and only while standing within it. Leaving suspends those benefits; returning before it ends restores them. The glyph does not move with the Sorcerer.

Spells cast from within the Ward gain +2 to their spell DC and cost 2 less ME, to a minimum cost of 1 ME. The Sorcerer need not commit Concentration to begin one of these spells, but must still occupy and resolve any Concentration required to maintain it. Damage cannot interrupt a spell the Sorcerer is casting from within the Ward. The Ward does not prevent attacks of opportunity, damage, grappling, forced movement, or any other consequence. The Sorcerer may create another Arcane Ward 1 hour after the previous Ward ends.

Aura of Negation

TALENT
Access: Barbarian
XP: 15 XP

After connecting with a melee attack, the Barbarian may spend 2 PE and activate Aura of Negation as part of that hit. Resolve the attack's ordinary damage normally. The Barbarian may instead use the Main Action to touch a willing adjacent creature.

Record the Barbarian's total Magic Resistance, including trained and equipment Magic Resistance, then roll percentile dice against it. Whether the roll succeeds or fails, the Barbarian's Magic Resistance becomes 0 for 1 minute and then returns normally.

On success, every ongoing beneficial or harmful magical effect on the touched creature is dispelled without a separate dispel check. Enchanted equipment, permanent item properties, inherent abilities, and effects explicitly impossible to dispel are unaffected.

Backstab

TALENT · MASTERY
Access: Rogue
XP: 5 XP

Sneak Attack, Backstab, Hamstring, and Dual Backstab form one package. They may work together when their individual requirements are met. Selecting another Job's Class Mastery suppresses every Rogue-Mastery benefit for that round. Finesse, Quick Draw, Casing, Underworld Courtesy, Skirmish, Envenom, and other non-Mastery features or talents remain available under their own rules.

If Sneak Attack or Backstab first becomes relevant through an opening attack, lock or choose Rogue Mastery immediately before that Attack roll. Rogue Mastery may therefore be selected during Round 0 when one of its abilities is otherwise legal.

Sneak Attack and Backstab each grant the same +1 Pen increase. These increases do not stack: an attack qualifying for both gains +1 Pen total, not +2.

Backstab. Backstab is a purchased Apprentice talent that modifies a melee attack made with a short, one-handed piercing weapon, normally a Dagger, Short Sword, Rapier, Rondel Dagger, Tanto, or comparable weapon. On a successful Backstab, add the Rogue Job level as fixed raw damage before Damage Reduction. This fixed damage is not multiplied by a critical hit or another dice multiplier. A qualifying Backstab gains +1 Pen, which does not stack with Sneak Attack's Pen increase. Backstab costs no PE, may operate during Round 0, and is not automatically Lethal Damage. It may combine with Sneak Attack when that attack independently satisfies Sneak Attack's requirements.

Bash

TALENT
Access: Mercenary; Barbarian
XP: 5 XP

Once per round, the character may spend 2 PE and replace one attack with Bash. The attacker makes a roll-under Strength check with BAB against the defender's choice of a roll-under Strength or Dexterity check with BAB.

On success, the defender becomes Prone. Success by 8 or more also permits the attacker to begin a grapple immediately without another attack or PE cost. The attacker drops held objects, follows the defender down, and continues with the grapple procedure. Failure by 8 or more makes the attacker Prone.

Both participants must have their feet on the ground. A one-category creature-size difference grants +4 to the larger participant. Bash cannot be used across a difference of two or more size categories. A trained Bash failure does not provoke an attack of opportunity.

See Combat — Hard Trip and Bash for the shared maneuver rules and untrained attempts.

Blind Fighting

TALENT
Access: Barbarian
XP: 10 XP

Blind Fighting provides combat awareness of creatures and dangers within 10 feet. Within that range, the character makes otherwise legal rolled active Defenses at -2 and attacks at -2. Blindness alone does not make the character Surprised or Flat-Footed against a threat perceived through Blind Fighting.

The 50% miss chance caused by Blindness or an unseen target does not apply within that 10-foot awareness. Blind Fighting does not remove an unrelated miss chance. Outside 10 feet, use the ordinary Blinded procedure unless another sense or ability provides awareness.

Blitzkrieg

TALENT
Access: Mercenary
XP: 15 XP

Developed by the Taslamaran military to break shield walls and punish exposed actions, Blitzkrieg is an immediate interrupt costing 3 PE. It may be used once per round.

When a perceived enemy within 30 feet begins a Main Action, attack, item use, spell, or Blitzkrieg, the Mercenary may respond before that action resolves. The Mercenary moves up to 15 feet without provoking attacks of opportunity and must end within melee reach of that enemy. A qualifying Quick Draw permission may draw an accessible weapon during this response. An action the Mercenary cannot perceive or recognize cannot trigger Blitzkrieg.

The Mercenary then makes one melee attack. If it hits and leaves at least 1 damage after protection, the target makes a Fortitude save against DC 10 + damage taken. Failure loses the triggering action. Success allows it to continue if it remains possible. The hit retains every other normal consequence, including the ordinary rules for interrupting a spell in progress.

Blitzkrieg may interrupt another Blitzkrieg. Resolve the most recently declared Blitzkrieg first, then work backward through the interrupted sequence while each remaining action is still possible. Ordinary Movement, free actions, and reactions other than Blitzkrieg do not trigger this Talent.

Brawling

TALENT
Access: Mercenary; Swashbuckler; Barbarian; Scout; Rogue; Bard; Shaman; Paladin; Hellion
XP: 10 XP

Prerequisite: Self Defense

Brawling allows the character to make an unarmed Full Attack and adds +0.5 Attack Speed to its Primary unarmed attack. Second Attack and Third Attack do not contribute.

Both hands must be empty or equipped only with qualifying fist weapons. These include knuckles, claws, katars, punch knives, and comparable weapons designed to function as extensions of unarmed combat. Armed and unarmed attacks cannot be mixed in a Full Attack receiving Brawling's benefit.

When Brawling bare-handed, each attack deals 1d6 bludgeoning damage with Pen 0. When using a qualifying Fist Weapon, use that weapon's printed damage profile.

A Brawling Full Attack pays the ordinary Full-Attack PE cost and no additional Brawling cost. A defender attempting to Parry a Brawling attack takes -8 Parry unless the defender possesses Enhanced Parry, in which case the penalty is -4.

Brawling does not remove Self Defense's -4 penalty when the Brawler uses a bare hand to Parry an armed attack.

Brew

TALENT
Access: Priest; Shaman; Sorcerer; Warlock; Necromancer
XP: 10 XP through Priest, Sorcerer, Warlock, or Necromancer; 15 XP through Shaman

Brew allows the character to place eligible spells into prepared potion vials. A spell can be brewed only when its spell entry explicitly marks it Brewable. The absence of that marker means that the spell cannot be brewed.

A potion affects the creature that drinks it. Choices that the spell would normally require are fixed during brewing unless the spell says otherwise. Anyone may drink a completed potion, and doing so costs no personal Magical Energy.

Brewing uses the shared Brew and Scribe Construction procedure below.

Brew and Scribe Construction

Brew and Scribe are separate Talents, but their construction follows the same procedure. Brew uses Wisdom and produces potions. Scribe uses Intelligence and produces scrolls.

The crafter must know and currently be able to cast every spell placed in the item. Neither Talent can store a Bard performance, Talent, spell-like ability, Ritual Spell, or True Necromancy.

Construction requires eight hours of focused work for each stored spell. The crafter invests each spell's normal Magical Energy cost during that work. After completing all of the work, make one construction check: a Wisdom check for Brew or an Intelligence check for Scribe. Apply every penalty from the item and its stored spells, then roll d20. A result equal to or below the adjusted Attribute succeeds.

Failure destroys the prepared blank and loses the work and Magical Energy invested in it.

Prepared Blanks

Every prepared vial or scroll blank has two independent ratings: Capacity and Potency. The artisan making the blank determines both. The brewer or scriber chooses an available blank rather than freely declaring its Potency.

When a spell is available through more than one Job, the crafter chooses which Job is being used to make the item. Use that Job's spell circle, Magical Energy cost, casting Attribute, ART, and caster level. An explicit non-Job casting route supplies the same values.

The stored caster level cannot be lower than the minimum caster level at which the chosen route grants the spell circle or higher than the crafter's current caster level through that route. For a Job using the standard nine-circle progression, the minimums for circles 1 through 9 are caster levels 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13, and 15.

Capacity is the caster-level load the blank can safely hold. Each spell must fit individually: its stored caster level cannot exceed the blank's Capacity. Add the stored caster levels together to find the item's total load. When several spells share one blank, each point of total load above Capacity imposes -1 on the construction check.

Standard blanks have a whole-number Capacity from 1 through 15. A blank has a base Typical Value of 5 silver pieces (sp) for each point of Capacity, then applies its Potency multiplier. Typical Value is an expected value under ordinary conditions rather than a guaranteed shop price. Capacity 13 through 15 blanks are normally Restricted and must be commissioned. Capacity above 15 exists only as an exceptional item with the DM's permission.

Craft (Glassworking) is used to make potion vials. Craft (Calligraphy and Illumination) or Craft (Bookbinding) is used to make scroll blanks. A suitable Craft Mastery is required to create a Strong or Overpowered blank. A trained craftsperson may create Weak and Normal blanks.

Using the Finished Item

Using a finished potion or scroll costs no personal Magical Energy. Each spell in a scroll is activated and consumed separately as a Main Action. Activating a scroll requires the Scrolls Talent or another explicit permission. Drinking a potion releases all of its stored spells together.

An instant effect remains instant. A spell with its own fixed duration keeps that duration unless its entry or the item says otherwise. A spell normally maintained by Concentration instead lasts 1 minute for each effective caster level. Apply Potency to this duration, rounding down to a minimum of 1 minute.

Example: An Elven Compound Potion

For this example, treat Cure Critical as a fourth-circle spell and Sanctuary as a second-circle spell. An elven brewer places two Cure Critical effects at caster level 6 and one Sanctuary effect at caster level 3 into the same potion. The total load is 15, so a Capacity 15 vial holds the spells without a Capacity penalty.

The highest spell circle imposes -3. The second and third stored spells impose another -8. With Wisdom 18, the brewer therefore succeeds on 7 or less using a Normal vial, 5 or less using a Strong vial, or 3 or less using an Overpowered vial.

Before adding the completed magic, the Capacity 15 vial has a Typical Value of 75 sp at Normal Potency, 225 sp at Strong Potency, or 750 sp at Overpowered Potency.

The work takes 24 hours and consumes the normal Magical Energy cost of all three spells. Drinking the completed potion releases all three effects together.

Counterstrike

TALENT
Access: Barbarian
XP: 15 XP

Prerequisite: Alertness

The first attack that hits the Barbarian during Round 0, or before the Barbarian has taken any offensive action in the battle, may trigger Counterstrike if its source is within the Barbarian's melee reach. The Barbarian must have attempted an active Defense against that attack; using the default static Defense does not permit Counterstrike.

Calculate the triggering attack's damage through Margin of Success and every applicable damage addition, but record it before applying the Barbarian's Damage Reduction or other mitigation. The triggering attack still resolves against the Barbarian normally.

The Barbarian then makes a melee attack against its source with a +5 bonus, using a held weapon or an unarmed attack. This response costs no PE and deals no weapon or unarmed damage of its own. It may be Dodged but cannot be Parried or Blocked.

On a hit, the source receives twice the recorded damage. The source's own Damage Reduction and other protection apply normally. Sanctuary, Presence, Spirit Aura, and comparable effects that would ordinarily prohibit attacking the source do not prevent Counterstrike.

The first qualifying attack is the only Counterstrike opportunity in that battle. Declining it or missing the +5 attack provides no later attempt.

Dance

TALENT · MASTERY
Access: Bard
XP: 5 XP

Activating Dance uses Movement and costs 2 PE. It lasts for a number of complete rounds equal to the Bard's Charisma modifier, with a minimum of one round. Before it expires, the Bard may renew it by spending Movement and 2 PE again.

Dance is a sequence of strange, broken-rhythm movements that makes the Bard difficult to read; it is neither ordinary social dancing nor required travel across the battlefield. The Bard gains a +4 Dodge-type bonus to Dodge and +4 Bluff. The Dodge-type bonus does not stack with Featherfoot or another Dodge-type bonus; use the greatest applicable bonus. The Bard may fold unusual movement into the sequence. If compatibility is genuinely uncertain, the DM may call for a Performance check.
