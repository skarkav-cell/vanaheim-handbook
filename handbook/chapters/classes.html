Understanding Classes and Jobs

Class Families

The four ordinary Class Families are Warrior, Adventurer, Acolyte, and Mage. A family groups Jobs that share a broad advancement chassis and occupies one of the four ordinary family positions available to a character.

Jobs

A Job is a level-bearing class within one Class Family. Each Job has its own level, unlock track, Job features, talent access, and any spell access printed in its profile.

The current core Jobs are:

Training Beyond the Core Jobs

Some organizations and traditions teach level-bearing paths beyond the thirteen core Jobs. Those paths belong to the separate GM and Beyond expansion; their individual requirements, progression, and interactions remain outside this Core chapter.

Talent Access and Exclusive Talents

Every named entry in a Job's rank table is a Talent, even when its name resembles an ordinary competency. A Talent may call for, modify, replace, or automatically resolve a Skill check, but that interaction does not turn the Talent into an ordinary Skill. Skills are governed separately by the Skills rules.

A character may learn a class talent only when one of their Jobs lists that talent, the talent explicitly lists their Job or Class Family as eligible, or another explicit rule grants access. Training supplies the method of learning an eligible talent; it does not grant access by itself.

An Exclusive talent may be learned only through its named Job unless an explicit rule says otherwise. Exclusive controls who may learn a talent. It is separate from Class Mastery, which controls whether a signature combat engine can operate alongside one learned from another Job.

Class Masteries

A Class Mastery is a Job's signature combat engine. A character can know Class Masteries from several Jobs but may use only one during a round.

A Class Mastery is available when the character knows it and the current circumstances allow it to affect a roll or action. The round's choice locks immediately before the first such roll or action. If only one Mastery is available, it locks automatically; if several are available, choose one. If the first relevant event is a Defense before the character's turn, lock or choose the Mastery before that Defense roll. A Mastery that becomes available later cannot replace the round's locked choice.

The choice governs the entire round. Apply the chosen package completely, including every applicable benefit, cost, requirement, limitation, penalty, movement permission, Attack Speed effect, duration rule, and ending consequence. Other Class Masteries provide no benefit during that round. An unchosen continuing Mastery supplies no benefits, but its existing costs, penalties, conditions, and other consequences remain.

Only explicitly tagged Class-Mastery abilities compete for this choice. Ordinary Job features, feats, spells, equipment, positioning bonuses, and untagged talents continue to function normally unless their own rules say otherwise. Choosing another Mastery never removes a cost, penalty, condition, or other consequence already created by an earlier activation.

Reading a Job Profile

Each Job profile opens with a prominent Job name and an Identity and Role passage explaining what the Job means in Vanaheim, its place in the world, and any requirements for taking it. If a Job has no special requirement, none is listed. Progression identifies the Class-Family Chassis and any other advancement rules. The Starting Package lists everything gained at Job level 1. The rank table lists every talent available through the Job; this chapter fully explains only the Job's signature talents, while complete rules and XP costs for the rest appear in Talents and Feats. Class Mastery, Spellcasting, and other exceptional Job rules follow when applicable. Not every Job uses every part.

Starting and Advancing

Starting at Level 1

A new character normally begins with one starting Job at Job level 1 and no unspent XP. The starting Job grants its Starting Package, its other level-1 features, and access to anything its profile makes available at that level.

Unless the Starting Package explicitly says otherwise, the character begins with Base Attack Bonus 0, base Fortitude 0, base Reflex 0, base Will 0, and no purchased advancement trains. An advancement train is a purchased permanent improvement to an ordinary attribute, a created Difficulty Class, Hit Points, Physical Energy, or Magical Energy; the complete train rules appear under Shared Advancement. Automatic family benefits and explicit racial or Job features are then applied normally.

Each Job tracks its own level. A character's Overall Level is the level of their highest Job; levels from different Jobs are never added together. The highest-level Job is the Main Job. If two Jobs are tied, the Job acquired first remains Main.

Acquiring level 1 in another Job later grants that Job's level-1 features and access, but it does not repeat skills, free selections, equipment, or other benefits reserved for a character's first Starting Package.

Buying Job Levels

To raise a Job by one level, pay XP equal to the new Job level. Raising a Job from level 5 to level 6 therefore costs 6 XP. No Job can advance beyond level 31.

The first level of an additional Job is a special entry rather than an automatic 1-XP purchase. It requires appropriate training or study and an entry cost set by the DM according to the Job, available instruction, circumstances, and how the character gains access to that training. After the Job has been entered, later levels use the ordinary new-level XP cost unless an explicit rule says otherwise.

Job levels unlock ranks, caps, talents, spells, and other access printed in the Job profile. They do not automatically purchase Base Attack Bonus, base saves, or advancement trains.

Example: Buying a Job Level and Entering Another Job

Raising Mercenary from level 5 to level 6 costs 6 XP. If that Mercenary later seeks level 1 in Rogue, the first Rogue level instead requires the DM-set entry cost and suitable training. Once Rogue level 1 has been acquired, raising Rogue to level 2 uses the ordinary cost of 2 XP.

Job-level advancement and talent instruction are separate requirements. A character may be able to self-train an eligible talent while still needing a mentor to acquire the next Job level.

Advancement Beyond Ordinary Levels

Job level 31 and Overall Level 31 are absolute ceilings. Reaching level 31 resolves every benefit the current rules legitimately grant for reaching that level, but there is no level 32 and no later level-derived HP, PE, ME, BAB cap, base-save cap, train capacity, caster level, spell-circle access, Talent access, or other progression.

After one Job reaches level 31 and the DM recognizes the character as a Grand Master, further exceptional advancement uses permanent Grandmaster Talents, or GM Talents. Record them on one character-wide list. A new GM Talent costs 10 XP for each GM Talent the character will know after learning it, and each entry states how many other GM Talents must already be known. There is no fixed GM Talent maximum and no separate capacity or Grandmaster-level track.

Several Jobs do not create several GM Talent counts or price ladders. Job-bound entries identify the Job they affect. The complete current procedure and initial catalogue belong to the separate GM and Beyond expansion.

Example: Advancement after Master Rank

Defeating a dragon might justify one further Warrior level after Master rank. Repeatedly defeating comparable dragons does not automatically justify every later level; the Warrior must meet a new challenge or achieve something that meaningfully extends their mastery.

Shared Advancement

Base Attack Bonus

Base Attack Bonus (BAB) is one global purchased value. Job levels unlock its cap but do not grant BAB automatically.

  • Each new point of BAB costs XP equal to the new value. Raising BAB from 5 to 6 therefore costs 6 XP.
  • The ordinary BAB cap is half the character's Overall Level, rounded down, reaching its maximum of 15 at Overall Level 30 or 31.
  • Reaching a higher cap gives permission to purchase BAB up to that value; it does not supply those ranks for free.
  • The DM may delay a BAB purchase when the character has not earned suitable combat training or development in the fiction. This changes access, not the numerical cap.

Combat Mastery is the explicit exception after Grand Master recognition. Each purchased entry requires displayed BAB at its current cap, raises the character's one displayed BAB by 1, and raises that cap by 1. It does not create a second BAB statistic.

Saving Throws

A base save is the permanent purchased Fortitude, Reflex, or Will value before its governing ability modifier and other bonuses. The highest Job level unlocks one universal base-save cap but grants no automatic increases.

  • Each new point of a favored base save costs XP equal to the new value.
  • Raising an unfavored base save costs one and one-half times the new value, rounded up. For example, raising an unfavored base save from 3 to 4 costs 6 XP.
  • Costs apply to each step rather than to the final value as a whole.

Fortitude, Reflex, and Will each have a cap equal to half the character's highest Job level, rounded down. Job level 30 or 31 therefore permits an ordinary purchased base-save value of 15 in all three saves.

A character's maximum HP is the total provided by Job benefits, purchased HP trains, worn armor, and other explicit effects.

The following automatic family benefits apply only when a Job reaches a new Overall Level as the Main Job. Secondary Job levels at or below the existing Overall Level do not grant another family progression, and a later Main-Job change never backfills those skipped levels.

Main Job familyAutomatic benefit at a qualifying new Overall Level
WarriorGain 1 HP at every qualifying Job level.
AdventurerGain 1 HP and 1 ME at every even Adventurer Job level.
MageGain 1 ME at every qualifying Job level.

When a character first acquires level 1 in any Acolyte-family Job, choose one of these permanent Acolyte progressions:

  1. gain 1 HP at every qualifying level in that Acolyte-family Job;
  1. gain 1 ME at every qualifying level in that Acolyte-family Job; or
  1. gain 1 HP and 1 ME at every even qualifying level in that Acolyte-family Job, using the same balanced HP-and-ME rate as an Adventurer.

Make this choice once rather than choosing between HP and ME at each level. If the Acolyte-family Job is secondary, choose the progression at that Job's level 1 even though it grants no per-level benefit until a later level in that Job establishes a new Overall Level.

For an ordinary single-Job character at level 30, these progressions provide Warrior +30 HP; Adventurer +15 HP and +15 ME; Acolyte +30 HP, +30 ME, or +15 HP and +15 ME; and Mage +30 ME.

Reaching a new Overall Level of 31 resolves the applicable level-31 family and PE gains once. No later Overall Level exists.

Maximum Physical Energy is:

3 + Constitution modifier + level-based PE + purchased PE trains + other explicit bonuses

PE cannot have a maximum below 0. Level-based PE follows the applicable schedule:

Ability Difficulty Classes and Caster Level

Caster level ordinarily increases through Jobs rather than being purchased. Unless an explicit Job, spell, or ability says otherwise, caster level equals half the relevant caster Job level, rounded down. Job level 5 therefore produces caster level 2, and Job level 30 or 31 produces ordinary caster level 15.

Spellcasting Mastery is an explicit Job-bound exception after Grand Master recognition and raises only the caster Job recorded on that purchase.

When a non-spell ability calls for a saving throw but does not supply a complete Difficulty Class, use:

10 + relevant ability modifier + half the relevant level, rounded down + ART + other explicit modifiers

Use the ability score named by the effect. If it names none, Charisma is the default. Determine the relevant level in this order:

  1. An effect with a complete explicit Difficulty Class formula uses that formula.
  1. A Job- or Class-Family-based ability uses the level in the Job or family that granted it. Because a character may have only one Job from each Class Family, Warrior DC, Adventurer DC, Acolyte DC, or Mage DC identifies one Job level.
  1. A generic non-spell ability that names no granting Job or Class Family uses Overall Level.

Spells use their separate default Difficulty Class:

10 + caster level + Charisma modifier + ART

An effect must still state its save type and what a successful save does unless another complete rule supplies that information. These formulas do not replace an opposed roll, roll-under check, maneuver procedure, or another ability's explicit resolution rule.

Multiple Jobs

One Job per Family

A character may possess no more than one Job from each Class Family.

Main and Secondary Jobs

The character's highest-level Job is automatically the Main Job. When Jobs are tied, the Job acquired first remains Main. Every other Job is a secondary Job.

Main Job determines advancement costs and which per-level benefits apply under the shared progression rules. It does not deactivate a secondary Job or erase abilities the character has learned.

The character's Overall Level is the level of the highest Job. Levels from different Jobs do not add together.

Advancing a Secondary Job

A secondary Job advances its own level, unlock track, talents, spells, and caps. It does not grant a second set of per-level HP, PE, ME, BAB, or free save increases.

When a secondary Job exceeds the former Main Job, it automatically becomes Main. Only the newly reached highest level receives the new Main Job's per-level benefit; earlier secondary levels are not recalculated or backfilled.

Class-Talent Access

A secondary Job can unlock talents when its own Job level and every other requirement qualify. The Talent Access and Exclusive Talents rule above still controls eligibility; an instructor cannot grant a Job-exclusive talent to a character who lacks the required Job.

Class Mastery and Multiclass Compatibility

Possessing several Jobs can provide several Class Masteries, but it does not allow their signature combat engines to stack. Use the one-Class-Mastery-per-round procedure above.

Warrior Jobs

Warrior Chassis

The Warrior family contains the Mercenary, Swashbuckler, and Barbarian Jobs. All three use the universal base-save cap and Warrior save costs under Shared Advancement, together with the Warrior train caps, 1 HP at every qualifying new Overall Level, and the Warrior PE schedule when the character possesses Endurance.

Once per battle, a Warrior may add their Warrior Job level to one otherwise legal active Dodge, Parry, or Block roll. This benefit cannot create a Defense the character could not otherwise choose and does not apply to a static Defense, which makes no roll.

Second Wind: once per battle, the Warrior may use the Main Action to recover HP equal to their Warrior Job level plus their Constitution modifier, with a minimum recovery of 1 HP. Movement remains available. Second Wind restores HP, not LP or Temporary HP, and cannot raise the character above their current maximum HP.

These two Warrior-Chassis abilities are unavailable during Round 0. They become available when Round 1 begins. This restriction does not prevent a Job ability, talent, or Class Mastery from functioning during Round 0 when its own rule permits it.

Each Warrior Job supplies its own Starting Package, Job features, Class Mastery, talent access, and weapon, armor, or shield training. Endurance, additional attacks, Attack Speed increases, Quick Draw, and equipment training apply only when an individual Job, talent, training, or other explicit rule grants them.

Mercenary

Identity and Role. Mercenaries are Vanaheim's professional soldiers: campaign veterans, household retainers, caravan guards, town watchmen, and sellswords whose loyalties differ but whose trade is trained combat. The name describes a broad military profession, not one company or allegiance. Mercenaries rely on extensive equipment training, dependable battlefield discipline, and practiced mastery of chosen weapons.

Progression. Mercenaries use Shared Advancement and the Warrior Chassis above. A Mercenary's ordinary Attack Speed is 1 for both armed and unarmed combat unless an explicit talent or other rule increases it.

Starting Package

Equipment Training. Acquiring Mercenary Job level 1 grants training with Axe, Bow, Crossbow, Dagger, Flail, Mace, Polearm, Sling, Spear, Staff, Sword, Whip, and unarmed combat. It also grants training with all ordinary shields and Light, Medium, and Heavy armor. This does not supply culturally restricted, specialist, item-specific, firearm, rifle, pistol, siege-weapon, or other explicitly exceptional training.

War Etiquette. The Mercenary gains +4 on Charisma-based social checks involving soldiers, guards, commanders, and other military-trained personnel.

Apprentice Talent Access. Bash and Enhanced Damage become available for purchase. Neither talent is learned automatically.

Swashbuckler

Identity and Role. Swashbucklers are duelists and mobile skirmishers who value speed, nerve, and individual technique over formation fighting. They use paired weapons, rapid footwork, and an opponent's failed attack to seize momentum, making them especially dangerous when they have room to move. Across Vanaheim, the title covers several traditions of individual swordsmanship rather than one uniform school. Swashbucklers appear among household champions, irregular companies, mobile officers, and professional duelists, where timing, footwork, nerve, and individual initiative matter more than formation discipline.

Progression. Swashbucklers use Shared Advancement and the Warrior Chassis above. A Swashbuckler's ordinary armed Attack Speed is 1 unless an explicit talent or other rule increases it.

Starting Package

Equipment Training. Acquiring Swashbuckler Job level 1 grants training with Dagger, one-handed Axe, Sword, Whip, Sling, Bow, Crossbow, and unarmed combat. It also grants training with Light armor and the Buckler. Specialist, culturally restricted, and item-specific equipment still requires its own training.

Sword Supremacy. While wielding two Sword-group weapons, the Swashbuckler gains +2 to Initiative and improves the critical-threat range of those swords by 1. When a Large or smaller opponent connects with a melee attack against the Swashbuckler, reduce its positive Attack Margin by 2, to a minimum of 0. This happens after the hit is established and cannot turn a hit into a miss. It reduces only the Margin added to damage; it is not Damage Reduction.

Suave. The Swashbuckler gains +4 on Charisma-based checks made to blend into a loosely organized group. Apply -4 instead when attempting to blend into a disciplined organization with an established order, such as an army or paladin order. This modifies the attempt to blend into the group, not every Charisma check involving its members.

Quick Draw. The Swashbuckler may draw an accessible one-handed Sword-group weapon without spending Movement. This remains available when another Class Mastery is selected. It does not accelerate sheathing or retrieving a stored weapon.

Apprentice Talent Access. Dirt Kick, Dual Wield, Heckle, Self Defense, and Juggle become available for purchase. None are learned automatically.

Dual Wield. Dual Wield is a separately purchased Apprentice talent. It remains available when another Class Mastery is selected.

  • Dual Wield requires two one-handed weapons. Choose one as the Primary Attack and the other as the offhand weapon. Each may be Light or Medium.
  • A Light offhand removes the ordinary two-weapon Attack and Parry penalties. A Medium offhand applies -2 Attack and -2 Parry to both weapons.
  • Every paired pass contains one Primary attack and one offhand attack. A single-attack Main Action still makes only one attack, using either weapon.
  • When both weapons can Parry, each supplies its ordinary base Parry use. A Medium offhand's -2 still applies to both weapons. Enhanced Parry adds its additional uses once to the shared Parry pool.
  • Second Attack grants a second paired pass every round, producing four attacks. The separately learned Third Attack talent provides no benefit while Dual Wield is being used.

Other Attack-Speed increases add unpaired attack passes only with the weapon to which they apply and follow the ordinary fractional schedule; they do not create another paired pass. A generally applicable increase may be assigned to either weapon. An increase granted by a particular weapon remains with that weapon.

Class Mastery: Greased Lightning

Greased Lightning is an Exclusive Mentor talent. It costs no PE and begins as part of a melee attack against an opponent. If the round's Class Mastery has not already been selected, choose Class Mastery: Swashbuckler immediately before that attack.

  • After each melee attack attempt against an opponent, gain a cumulative +2 to Attack and active Defense rolls, to a maximum of +6. Each increase applies after that attack resolves and therefore affects subsequent rolls. It never adds to a static Defense.
  • After each individual melee attack, including each hand's attack within a paired pass, the Swashbuckler may move 5 feet without provoking an attack of opportunity from that movement. This may carry the Swashbuckler beyond ordinary Speed during the round.
  • Upon reaching +6, the full-buildup additional attack begins at the start of the next round. It does not enter a Full Attack already in progress.
  • Full buildup grants one additional single-weapon attack each round as part of a Full Attack, with no additional PE cost. It is not another complete paired pass.
  • While using Dual Wield, make the first additional attack with the Primary Attack, the next round's with the offhand weapon, then continue alternating. A new buildup begins again with the Primary Attack.
  • The cumulative bonus and full-buildup benefit remain until Greased Lightning ends.
  • Greased Lightning ends after three consecutive rounds in which the Swashbuckler makes no melee attack against an opponent. Choosing another Job's Class Mastery ends Greased Lightning immediately.

A Swashbuckler with Dual Wield, Second Attack, and full Greased Lightning makes five attacks and reaches the ordinary Full-Attack limit.

Only Greased Lightning, Wolverine Stance, and Tornado Slash use Class Mastery: Swashbuckler. Learning Greased Lightning does not grant the other two talents or waive their individual requirements and costs. Sword Supremacy, Quick Draw, Dual Wield, Finesse, Riposte, Cross-Slash, Hedgehog Stance, Featherfoot, and the remaining Swashbuckler talents do not require this Mastery unless their own rules explicitly say otherwise.

Example: Dual Wield, Second Attack, and Greased Lightning

A Swashbuckler begins with Attack Speed 1. A Full Attack therefore supplies one attack pass. After purchasing Dual Wield, that pass contains two attacks—Primary and offhand—without becoming two separate passes. After purchasing Second Attack, the Swashbuckler receives a second complete paired pass every round and therefore makes four attacks: Primary and offhand on the first pass, then Primary and offhand on the second.

Suppose the Swashbuckler begins Greased Lightning with the first Primary attack of Round 1. That attack receives no accumulated Greased Lightning bonus. After it resolves, the Swashbuckler gains +2 Attack and active Defense and may move 5 feet. The first offhand attack gains +2; after it resolves, the bonus rises to +4 and another 5-foot movement becomes available. The second Primary attack gains +4; after it resolves, the bonus reaches +6 and another 5-foot movement becomes available. The final offhand attack gains +6 and permits one more 5-foot movement after it resolves.

Reaching +6 during Round 1 does not insert another attack into that Full Attack. At the start of Round 2, full buildup becomes active. The two paired passes still supply four attacks, and Greased Lightning adds one unpaired Primary attack, producing five attacks for the round. Every attack gains the accumulated +6 and permits its own optional 5-foot movement after it resolves. In Round 3, the fifth attack uses the offhand weapon; later rounds continue alternating until Greased Lightning ends.

Third Attack would add nothing while Dual Wield is being used. Greased Lightning supplies the fifth attack through its own explicit rule rather than by granting another paired pass or changing each weapon's Attack Speed.

Maintaining a Song

  • Starting a Song uses the Bard's Main Action and pays its starting ME cost. Maintaining it uses a Free Action each round. Pay ME only when the listed upkeep interval comes due: a per-minute Song still requires the Free Action every round but does not charge ME every round. A pulsing Song applies its first pulse immediately; the Bard must maintain it and pay the next upkeep before each later pulse.
  • The Bard may maintain only one Song at a time. Starting another immediately ends the former Song. A Song may begin during Round 0.
  • Voice alone is sufficient. Singing leaves the Bard's hands free but prevents speech, warnings, commands, and verbal spell components. An instrument occupies the hands needed to play it but permits speech. Switching between voice and instrument ends the performance; restarting requires another Main Action and starting cost.
  • Maintaining a Song does not use spell Concentration. Damage alone does not end it. The Bard may end it voluntarily. It ends automatically if the Bard cannot continue performing, cannot take the maintenance Free Action, cannot pay the next upkeep, or becomes Stunned, Paralyzed, or Unconscious.
  • Unless stated otherwise, a Song has a continuously updating 60-foot radius centered on the Bard. A creature is affected only while it remains in that area and can hear the Song. Losing either condition ends that creature's effect immediately.
  • Hearing is required for beneficial and hostile Songs. Covering the ears grants +2 on saves; effective earplugs ordinarily grant +4 to +8 according to quality and fit; Deafness grants immunity. Direct notes, Songs of Silence's field, and Songs of Antimagic's effect on existing magic are explicit exceptions.
  • Resolve a hostile Song's applicable Magic Resistance and save once when a creature is first exposed. That result lasts for the continuous performance; leaving and returning does not grant another attempt. Songs of Antimagic's casting gate and Songs of Sleep's second save are explicit exceptions.
  • Identical Songs do not stack; use the strongest.

Dance

Activating Dance uses Movement and costs 2 PE. It lasts for a number of complete rounds equal to the Bard's Charisma modifier, with a minimum of one round. Before it expires, the Bard may renew it by spending Movement and 2 PE again.

Dance is a sequence of strange, broken-rhythm movements that makes the Bard difficult to read; it is neither ordinary social dancing nor required travel across the battlefield. The Bard gains a +4 Dodge-type bonus to Dodge and +4 Bluff. The Dodge-type bonus does not stack with Featherfoot or another Dodge-type bonus; use the greatest applicable bonus. Enhanced Dodge expressly adds its +1.

Its Dodge benefit is visual, mind-affecting misdirection. It provides no Dodge bonus against a mindless attacker, an attacker immune to mind-affecting effects, or an attacker unable to perceive the movement. Immobilization or an incompatible physical action ends Dance.

The Bard may fold unusual movement into the sequence. If compatibility is genuinely uncertain, the DM may call for a Performance check. Dance itself permits that specific check without requiring a separately learned Performance field and grants +4 on it. The bonus applies only to incorporating the uncertain action into Dance; it does not improve another Performance field, a Song or direct-note DC, or another Bard ability. Dance may coexist with one Song and activates that Song's printed Dance effect.

Direct Notes

Each direct note deals a number of d6 of magical sonic damage equal to half the Bard Job level, rounded down, with a minimum of 1d6. Complete the nominal dice expression, roll no more than four damage dice, and replace each excess die with +2. Bard level 30 therefore produces 4d6 + 22, not 15 rolled dice. Converted +2 values remain part of the damage-dice subtotal for critical multiplication if an explicit later rule ever permits a critical; the direct notes themselves make no Attack roll and cannot critically hit.

Magic Resistance applies. Magic Protection and worn physical armor's flat DR reduce the damage. Direct notes use no Penetration / Armor Grade comparison, Attack Margin, or critical hit.

True Necromancy

True Necromancy is a freeform Abyssal working rather than an ordinary fixed spell. It becomes available when the Necromancer gains first-circle spell access. A working can be a prepared ritual lasting hours or days, a repeatable ritual already settled with the DM, or an improvised plea attempted with a Main Action.

The Necromancer states the desired result, wording, casting time, preparation, and deliberate Offerings. An Offering may be a life, goods, a relationship, or another sacrifice whose loss has genuine value to the people involved. The DM judges that value and whether it is proportionate to the request; there is no universal price table.

The DM assigns an Equivalent Spell Circle from the working's scope. The Necromancer's highest accessible circle is the expected scope, not an absolute prohibition: an attempt beyond it may be allowed with dramatically worse and potentially undisclosed odds or terms. A working beyond the ordinary spell scale remains wholly DM-adjudicated and gains no automatic higher circle.

Dark Points measure accumulated personal corruption on a scale from 0 to 10. The Control Roll below may add them; their complete consequences and recovery appear under Dark Points.

Resolve the working in this order:

  1. Set Control Chance. It begins at the Necromancer's Charisma score as a percentage. The DM adjusts it for scope, preparation, wording, casting time, Offerings, and circumstances, and need not disclose the final number.
  1. Roll Control. Roll d100. A result equal to or below the Control Chance keeps the working under the Necromancer's control and adds no Dark Point. On a higher result, the Abyss may distort, overfulfill, or exact another price. The DM determines the outcome and whether the Necromancer gains any Dark Points and, if so, how many.
  1. Consume Offerings. Offerings are consumed regardless of the Control result and are not returned by later complications.
  1. Check World Rejection. When the Counter Force can perceive the working, roll d10. If the result is equal to or below the Equivalent Spell Circle, roll d20 for severity and let the DM determine how reality suppresses, redirects, exposes, or complicates the working.

Control and World Rejection are independent. A controlled working can still be rejected, and an uncontrolled working can escape notice. Offerings remain consumed and Dark Points remain gained even when World Rejection prevents the intended result.

Being inside a Reality Marble does not by itself conceal the working from the Counter Force or exempt it from World Rejection. Apply the same perception condition and check. Any protective or concealing effect depends on a specific established mechanism. The marble's own rules may additionally prohibit, alter, or punish Necromancy. Control, Offerings, Dark Points, and Abyssal consequences continue to apply.

Dark Points

Whenever a resolution raises the Dark Point total to 4 or higher, roll d10. If the result is equal to or below the current Dark Points, the Abyss claims the character; the character becomes unplayable and passes to the DM. A total of 10 guarantees this result.

Dark Points can fade through prolonged abstinence from Necromancy. Because all magic personally invoked by a Necromancer is translated into Abyssal Necromancy, ordinary translated spells also interrupt the interval:

Current Dark PointsOrdinary interval before a recovery roll
1About one month
2About six months
3About one to two years
4 or moreAn increasingly long interval set by the DM

When the interval passes without the Necromancer personally invoking any Necromancy, roll d10. A result greater than the current Dark Points removes one point. Any other result removes none, and another full interval must pass before trying again.

Converting an Existing Mage Job

When Sorcerer or Warlock becomes Necromancer, convert that Job in place:

  1. Preserve its Job level, rank, Job XP, acquisition order, Main- or secondary-Job status, and tie precedence. Do not charge another Job-entry cost.
  1. Preserve Overall-Level history, purchased BAB, saving throws, advancement trains, PE, ME, current and maximum ME, and every earlier Mage-Chassis benefit. Recalculate only values normally derived from the renamed Job, such as the displayed Job name and caster level. Conversion grants no refill, refund, backfill, duplicate chassis benefit, or free train reselection.
  1. Preserve learned Metamagic abilities and Ritual Spells. Conversion grants no additional selection and no free reselection.
  1. Preserve every spell actually learned as a legacy spell. It becomes Necromantic when the character invokes it. The former Job's spell list closes for future learning; new spells must come from the Necromancer list or another explicit source.
  1. Preserve purchased generic talents, but close the former Job's access ladder for future purchases. A purchased Sorcerer Arcane Ward or Mana Shield remains as a legacy talent and becomes Necromantic when used; neither enters the Necromancer ladder. When the converted Necromancer uses either retained talent, references to the Sorcerer in its procedure mean that converted character; every other part of the procedure remains unchanged.
  1. Preserve mundane equipment training. Do not grant the Necromancer level-1 equipment package during conversion. Former Job-specific magical permissions end unless this profile explicitly preserves them; Warlock's trained-Light-armor Spell Failure waiver therefore ends.

Conversion is ordinarily one-way. Reversing it requires an exceptional DM-approved event and is not ordinary retraining or Job switching.

Example: A Warlock becoming a Necromancer

A level-12 Warlock is converted in place. The character becomes a level-12 Necromancer, retaining the same Job XP, rank, acquisition order, Main- or secondary-Job status, and tie precedence. Caster level remains 6, and Job level 12 still permits spell access through fourth circle.

The character keeps purchased BAB and saves, advancement trains, PE, current and maximum ME, every earned Mage-Chassis benefit, learned Metamagic abilities, Ritual Spells, purchased talents, and mundane equipment training. Learned Warlock spells remain as legacy spells and become Necromantic when invoked. The former Warlock spell list and talent ladder no longer grant future choices; new spells and talent purchases must use Necromancer access or another explicit source.

The conversion grants no Necromancer Starting Package, ME refill, refund, backfill, additional Metamagic selection, or free reselection. Light-armor training remains, but the Warlock-only Spell Failure waiver ends, so the armor's listed Spell Failure applies when the character casts Necromancer spells.
