Equipment

Equipment at a Glance

An equipment profile keeps an item's rules, handling, weight, Typical Value, and Availability together. Weapons list Damage and Armor Penetration (Pen); armor lists Armor Grade (Grade), Damage Reduction (DR), and Armor HP; shields list the defensive bonuses they provide. Pen is compared with Grade to determine how much physical armor DR applies, while Armor HP increases the wearer's maximum HP rather than measuring the armor's structural durability. Typical Value is a comparison point, not a guaranteed shop price, and Availability shows how readily an item can normally be found. A specific item's entry controls any exception to these general rules.

Money, Markets, and Availability

Silver-Equivalent Accounting

Ten copper equal one silver, and ten silver equal one gold. Equipment uses silver as its ordinary reference value, so prices, treasure, and foreign coins can be compared without requiring players to maintain several parallel accounting systems.

Record the mint or origin of a character's money when it matters. The character may still total that money by its silver-equivalent value: a pouch can contain Taslamaran crowns or Imperial deniers while the character sheet records what the collection is ordinarily worth in silver.

Silver-equivalent accounting does not guarantee that every coin will be accepted at that value. A merchant may weigh, discount, exchange, or refuse an unfamiliar or distrusted mint.

As approximate economic comparisons, one copper is about an ordinary laborer's daily earnings, one silver is about a skilled artisan's weekly earnings, and one gold is about ten weeks of skilled work. These are guides to scale, not fixed wages or modern-currency conversions.

Typical Value and Variable Prices

An item's Typical Value is its expected silver-equivalent value under ordinary conditions. It is not a guaranteed shop price.

Region, scarcity, war, quality, legality, demand, and coin reputation can change the actual price or make an item unavailable. The DM determines the local result from the situation; there is no universal market-price modifier table.

Catalogue values are based on the labor, materials, construction, and availability appropriate to Vanaheim.

Availability and Scarcity

Equipment can carry one of four availability labels:

  • Common: normally present in ordinary trade where the item is culturally and practically appropriate.
  • Limited: obtainable through the right market, craft, supplier, or modest search, but not reliably stocked everywhere.
  • Rare: normally requires a specialist source, connection, substantial search, or unusual opportunity.
  • Restricted: controlled, prohibited, politically sensitive, or limited to authorized buyers. An item can be Restricted without being physically rare.

Availability communicates an item's position in the market. It does not establish a public DC, settlement requirement, waiting period, or guarantee of access. When acquisition requires mechanical resolution, the DM may use a private contact, fixer, or fence procedure.

Some exceptional armor and shields are marked Nationally controlled instead of receiving an ordinary market label. They are priceless for catalogue purposes, have no public Typical Value, and cannot be acquired merely by offering sufficient silver.

Equipment Categories and Properties

A category is part of the item's basic classification. For example, Light, Medium, and Heavy establish the ordinary starting Pen of a weapon and the ordinary starting Grade of armor. A category does not automatically determine every other field.

A property is a defined rule attached to a particular item. It can supply special handling, an alternate use, a restriction, or an exception. A property changes only what its text states; its name does not imply additional rules.

Requirements and Restrictions

A requirement identifies something needed for a listed use, such as a minimum ability, training, free hands, ammunition, or a particular situation. A restriction identifies a use that is unavailable, such as a bow that cannot be fired while mounted.

The item entry states the consequence of failing a requirement or meeting a restriction. Only the listed consequences apply; an additional penalty or reduced-use option requires an explicit rule.

Item-Specific Exceptions

A specific item can override a general rule by stating the exception in its own profile. The exception is narrow: permission to use one bow while mounted does not change other longbows, and a weapon that qualifies for one special maneuver does not grant that qualification to its entire category.

When an item profile and a universal procedure appear to conflict, apply the item text only to the exact value or use it changes. The remaining universal procedure still applies.

Readily Accessible Equipment

Half the character's Dexterity score, rounded down, is the practical guideline for how much equipment can be kept readily arranged on the body. Treat this as a guideline, not an exact slot limit.

Worn clothing and armor do not consume this capacity. Weapons, component satchels, potion belts, packs, and comparable arranged equipment do. An ordinary item counts as at least one; a bulky or awkward item may count as two, three, or more at the DM's judgment and never counts as a fraction.

A suitable container counts according to its own bulk rather than making every small object inside it count separately. Its contents are Stored unless the container explicitly makes a particular item Accessible. A backpack can count as more than one.

Worn, Stored, and Secured Equipment

  • Held: The item is already in hand and requires no retrieval.
  • Accessible: The item is arranged for immediate retrieval.
  • Stored: The item is inside an ordinary pack or container.
  • Secured: The item is locked, tied down, buried, or deeply packed. It is normally unavailable during combat without the time or circumstances the DM requires.

Drawing, Stowing, Retrieving, and Dropping Items

Bringing one Accessible item to hand uses Movement and does not itself provoke an attack of opportunity. A character with BAB +1 or higher may bring any one Accessible item to hand as part of ordinary movement. Without BAB +1, bringing the item to hand consumes Movement and provides no movement through that same allowance.

Retrieving a Stored item uses the Main Action and provokes an attack of opportunity while threatened. Retrieving a Secured item requires whatever additional time or circumstances the DM determines.

Dropping an item held in hand is free. Sheathing, holstering, or otherwise putting away a weapon uses Movement and provokes an attack of opportunity while threatened. An explicit Quick Draw or item rule can create a narrower exception.

Light, Medium, and Heavy Weapons

Light, Medium, and Heavy are formal weapon categories. Their ordinary baseline Pen values are:

Weapon categoryBaseline Pen
Light1
Medium2
Heavy3

Category and handedness are separate. A non-Heavy weapon may still require two hands, and using two hands does not make it Heavy. The item's profile supplies both fields. A Heavy Weapon made for the wielder's size occupies both hands in ordinary use; the weapon-size procedure below can change a wrong-sized weapon's hand requirement without changing its category.

A Heavy Weapon has no universal Attack Speed or Parry penalty and is not restricted to Full Attacks. Its usual tradeoffs are occupying both hands, its physical bulk, and the inability to use a shield at the same time. Only a weapon formally classified as Heavy and wielded in both hands qualifies for Heavy Attack.

Armor Penetration (Pen)

Armor Penetration, abbreviated Pen, measures a weapon's ability to defeat Grade-sensitive physical armor. Pen does not add directly to Attack or damage.

Each weapon entry lists its Pen. Light, Medium, and Heavy weapons ordinarily begin at Pen 1, 2, and 3, but material, enchantment, construction, ammunition, or an explicit property can change the final value. Heavy Attack adds +1 Pen for that attack.

Compare final Pen against the protection's Armor Grade using Damage Reduction, Armor Penetration, and Armor Grade in Combat. Grade-less protection such as Energy Armor and Supernatural DR applies without this comparison unless its own rule says otherwise.

One-Handed and Two-Handed Weapons

A one-handed weapon occupies one hand. Wielding one leaves the other hand available for a shield, item, potion, spell component, or another legal use, but grants no universal numerical bonus.

A two-handed weapon made for the wielder's size requires both hands for ordinary use. It cannot be used at the same time as a shield, and using two hands does not by itself increase Pen or make the weapon Heavy. A two-handed weapon can make a single attack or a Full Attack normally. The weapon-size procedure below can change the hand requirement without changing category.

A long or two-handed weapon may be physically impractical in cramped surroundings. This is situational adjudication, not a universal weapon penalty. An explicit technique or item property may provide an exception.

The Longsword is a trained exception. Untrained two-handed use is Medium and Pen 2. A trained wielder may use it one-handed as Medium and Pen 2, or two-handed as Heavy and Pen 3, permitting Heavy Attack.

The Elven Thinblade is a separate exception. It is a Medium, one-handed weapon with Pen 2. Using it in two hands raises its Pen to 3, but it remains Medium and does not permit Heavy Attack. The Katana does not share either increase: training can permit one-handed use, but it remains Medium and Pen 2 in either grip.

Weapon Size and Oversized Weapons

Weapon category is not weapon size. Light, Medium, and Heavy describe the weapon's rules category; an unusually large weapon is a separate question.

The core catalogue lists weapons made for Medium creatures. A creature can ordinarily use a weapon made for a creature one size category larger or smaller, with the following changes:

  • A one-handed weapon made one size larger requires two hands. A weapon that already requires two hands becomes unusable unless an explicit ability permits its use.
  • A two-handed weapon made one size smaller can be used in one hand. A one-handed weapon made one size smaller remains one-handed.
  • A wrong-sized weapon applies -2 Attack and -2 Parry with that weapon. It does not penalize Dodge or a shield Block.
  • A weapon made for a creature two or more size categories larger or smaller is unusable unless an explicit rule says otherwise.

Changing weapon size changes its damage dice and ordinary hand requirement only. It does not automatically change Category, Pen, Critical Threat, Reach, or eligibility for Heavy Attack. Heavy Attack still requires a formally Heavy weapon wielded in both hands.

A specially constructed weapon, such as a giant hammer or cleaver, may list exceptional Category, Pen, Reach, requirements, or other properties in its own profile. Weapon size has no universal weight formula; use the printed item weight or the DM's ruling when the distinction matters.

Creature size, a talent, a spell, or another explicit effect can modify the final threatened area without changing the weapon's listed statistics.

Weapon Properties and Special Handling

Properties record rules that do not belong in the core profile fields. Each property must be defined before it is used in the catalogue.

A melee weapon with a thrown mode appears once in the melee catalogue. Its property records the thrown Effective Range and any handling change. A qualification such as suitability for a mounted-charge damage benefit likewise applies only when the weapon or another explicit rule states it.

Requirements involving Strength, training, bracing, unusual ammunition, restricted grips, or special environments appear in the weapon's properties or dedicated entry. A historical weapon name alone establishes no additional requirement or handling rule.

  • Trip +2 or Disarm +2: Add +2 to the wielder's adjusted roll-under threshold when using that weapon for the named maneuver. The property does not grant maneuver training or change the maneuver's action cost, PE cost, untrained penalty, or once-per-round limit.
  • Brace: When a Readied attack with the weapon resolves against a creature charging the wielder and connects, double the weapon's damage dice for that hit. Modifiers are not doubled.
  • Charge Weapon: The weapon qualifies for doubled weapon dice after a straight, clear mounted Charge of at least 30 feet. This is separate from the +1 Pen applied to every legal mounted Charge.
  • Thrown: A property such as Thrown 20 lets the weapon make a ranged attack with an Effective Range of 20 feet. Each thrown weapon must be brought to hand through the ordinary drawing rules or an explicit Quick Draw exception.
  • Trained: The listed use requires relevant weapon training from a class, ability, instructor, or established character training. It is not a universal feat category.

Vorpal

Vorpal is a magical weapon property, not a material. The weapon retains its underlying material and every modifier supplied by that material. Vorpal adds +3 Pen to the finished weapon profile after the base weapon and material are resolved.

Each Vorpal weapon states its own magical Attack and damage bonus and its Vorpal trigger range. A +3 Vorpal weapon has Critical Threat 17–20; its +3 Attack and +3 damage are separate from the Vorpal property's +3 Pen.

When an attack with that +3 Vorpal weapon connects on a natural 17–20, resolve its ordinary critical and then apply one Vorpal result:

  • A Medium or smaller creature with a severable head is beheaded.
  • Against a Large or larger creature, add +50 flat damage to the attack instead of beheading it. This damage is part of the same hit, is not multiplied by the ordinary critical, and then follows the attack's normal protection and damage-routing procedure.
  • A creature without a severable head cannot be beheaded; the Vorpal property adds no substitute damage unless its own item entry says otherwise.

When a manufactured weapon successfully Parries an attack made with a Vorpal weapon or Dimensional Blade, its wielder makes a Reflex save to protect the parrying weapon. No comparison between the two weapons' Pen, Grade, or material is required to trigger the save.

Dimensional Blade uses the caster's Spell DC. Vorpal uses the attacking wielder's standard non-spell DC:

10 + ability modifier governing that attack + half Overall Level, rounded down + ART

Resolve a failed save through the parrying weapon's underlying material:

  • An ordinary, masterwork, silver, cold-iron, or comparable steel weapon becomes Broken.
  • A Tanso or Mithril weapon becomes Damaged and takes -2 Attack. Another failed save under this procedure while it remains so Damaged makes it Broken.
  • An Adamantine weapon is unaffected.

Magical enchantment alone grants no material protection. A magical steel weapon remains steel for this procedure.

Vorpal does not make the weapon's material irrelevant, grant unlimited Pen, or by itself resize the weapon. A protection can name Vorpal as an explicit bypass.

Example: Molydeus Guardian Axe.

Effective, Far, and Extreme Range

Every physical ranged weapon lists an Effective Range.

  • Effective: From the attacker out to the listed Effective Range. The attack takes no range penalty and resolves damage normally.
  • Far: Beyond Effective Range and out to three times Effective Range. The attack takes -8 Attack and resolves damage normally.
  • Extreme: Beyond Far Range and out to five times Effective Range. The attack takes -16 Attack, cannot make a called shot, and adds no positive Attack Margin to damage.
  • Beyond Extreme: The attack is unavailable.

These multipliers give total distance from the attacker rather than additional segments. A weapon-specific rule can change its own range or limit.

Armor and Shields

Reading an Armor or Shield Profile

Armor remains Light, Medium, or Heavy for training and category-dependent rules. Its profile contains the following values:

FieldMeaning
GradeThe Armor Grade used when an attack's Pen is compared with the armor.
DRThe armor's Grade-sensitive physical Damage Reduction after that comparison.
Armor HPThe amount the armor adds to the wearer's maximum HP while worn. This represents protected tolerance, not damage to the item.
MobilityA penalty imposed where the armor interferes with movement or active physical response.
Spell FailureThe chance that somatic casting fails when the caster's job, faith, talent, or other source does not permit that armor.
WeightThe armor's weight in pounds.
Typical ValueIts ordinary silver-equivalent comparison value, including the complete profile and initial fitting.
AvailabilityIts ordinary position in the market; this does not guarantee local access.

Nationally controlled exceptional armor and shields deliberately omit Typical Value. Their Availability identifies the controlling nation or institution instead of implying an ordinary market.

Light, Medium, and Heavy Armor

Light armor begins at Grade 1, Medium armor at Grade 2, and Heavy armor at Grade 3. A specific armor can depart from its category baseline without changing category.

Medium and Heavy armor do not universally reduce Speed. Heavy armor limits an ordinary Run to three times Speed and retains the established grapple Physical Energy costs. Armor creates no additional universal Physical Energy cost per round.

Armor Grade (Grade)

Armor Grade, abbreviated Grade, represents physical armor's resistance to penetration. Compare the attack's Pen with Grade before applying the armor's DR, as described in Combat. Grade does not add directly to Defense or Armor HP.

Damage Reduction

An armor's DR is the physical Damage Reduction adjusted by the Pen-versus-Grade comparison. Grade-less protection, such as Energy Armor or Supernatural DR, follows its own flat procedure rather than using the worn armor's Grade.

Armor Hit Points

Armor HP increase the wearer's maximum HP while the armor is worn. Once added, those points belong to the same pool as HP from Jobs, training, and other sources: damage and healing do not track them separately. They are not structural damage to the armor.

Armor Penalties, Requirements, and Movement

Mobility applies to Dodge, Reflex saves, the initial Grab Attack, and physical movement or Dexterity-based checks when the armor materially interferes. It does not reduce ordinary Speed.

Armor training can come from a job, talent, faith, or instruction received in play. An untrained wearer doubles the armor's Mobility penalty and applies that resulting penalty to Attack and every active Defense: Dodge, Parry, and Block. The armor still supplies its Grade, DR, and Armor HP.

Spell Failure applies when the character's casting source does not permit somatic casting in the worn armor. Whether a particular job, faith, talent, or other source waives it belongs to that source. When Spell Failure occurs, the spell expends its Magical Energy and is lost.

One worn armor profile supplies Grade, DR, Armor HP, Mobility, and Spell Failure. Its ordinary padding, mail joints, helmet, gauntlets, and other integrated components are already represented. Multiple armor profiles do not stack unless an explicit item says otherwise.

An armor profile assumes its ordinary head protection. A restrictive closed helmet gives -2 to sight- and hearing-based Perception while closed. Opening, closing, putting on, or removing that restrictive portion uses Movement. Helmets use no separate Grade, DR, Armor HP, hit-location, or called-shot-to-the-head rules.

Armor Fitting, Donning, and Removal

Armor is fitted to its intended wearer. When an armor has a Typical Value, that value includes its complete listed profile and initial fitting. Flexible armor may sometimes transfer between wearers of sufficiently similar build, at the DM's discretion. Rigid or articulated armor—especially breastplates and plate harnesses—must be properly refitted before it provides its full listed profile to a different wearer. Refitting time and cost depend on the item and circumstances; there is no universal refitting percentage or ill-fitting-armor penalty table.

Shields and Shield Bonuses

A carried shield supplies its listed bonus to Block and permits a separate base Block use. Projectile Block is the shield's total Block bonus against ordinary arrows, bolts, and similar projectiles; it replaces the normal shield bonus for that Defense.

Add applicable bonuses from training, materials, and magical Accuracy once each. A material bonus already included in a finished shield profile is not added again.

A shield's Reflex bonus applies when the wielder can reasonably interpose it against a directional effect. This does not spend a Block use and provides no benefit when the shield cannot protect the wielder from that effect.

A shield's printed Mobility penalty follows the same application areas as armor Mobility while the shield is carried.

Shields provide no inherent Grade, DR, or Armor HP and grant no universal shield-bash attack. A feat, talent, spell, or explicit item can create an exception. Readying or removing a shield uses Movement.

Shields may receive permanent improvements through the Enchant and Consecrate spell families. The individual spell determines the available benefits and how they apply.

Weapon and Armor Material Comparisons

Material Weaknesses

Silver and cold iron reduce the ordinary weapon profile's Pen by 1, to a minimum of Pen 0. They change neither category nor damage dice.

Elves, dark elves, and sprites possess Cold-Iron Weakness. Werewolves and creatures whose profiles identify the weakness possess Silver Weakness.

When the appropriate material hits a creature weak to it:

  1. Ignore any protection whose own rule names that material as its bypass.
  2. Apply every other relevant DR normally.
  3. Add half the remaining damage, rounding down.
  4. Apply eligible Physical Energy mitigation.
  5. Route the remainder through HP and LP normally.

If DR reduces the damage to 0, the weakness creates no damage. Other explicit material weaknesses can use the same procedure.

Tanso Steel

Tanso is a refined Vanaheim crucible steel. It is more homogeneous, lighter, and more durable than ordinary steel and accepts enchantment readily.

Tanso equipment is frequently masterwork, but the material does not automatically confer that quality. Its increased physical durability can matter when exceptional harm threatens the item; Tanso otherwise uses the normal Damaged and Broken rules.

Mithril

Mithril is well suited to fine edges, flexible armor, and curved weapons. A functional Mithril item requires specialized tools, furnaces, and exceptional skill; producing one ordinarily takes about twice the work of a comparable steel item. The extra work affects value and availability but sets no fixed crafting time.

Mithril shields gain +2 Block and +2 Projectile Block. They weigh half as much as ordinary shields and, because they are automatically masterwork, improve Mobility by 1 toward 0. Mithril grants no additional Reflex bonus.

Adamantite

Adamantite requires extraordinary heat comparable to an arc furnace. Losache Keep and major dwarven forges can produce it repeatedly at their limited scale. Exceptional elven magic may create the necessary conditions for an individual work without creating an ordinary production industry.

Adamantine armor retains ordinary weight because the finished harness uses less material to achieve its protection. Adamantine shields gain +3 Block and +3 Projectile Block. They retain ordinary weight and Mobility. Adamantite grants no additional Reflex bonus.

EquipmentEnergy Armor, Supernatural DR, and Special Construction

Adamantite is not ordinarily destructible through direct destruction, disintegration, or material-transformation magic. Unless an effect explicitly overrides this protection, an effect that would destroy or transform an ordinary item instead damages only an adamantine surface layer and cannot make the item Broken. Such an effect can still affect an unprotected creature within adamantine armor when the creature is independently a legal target.

Crystalbrittle supplies one explicit implementation: it forms a brittle crystalline coating and applies one nonstacking Damaged result without transforming, breaking, killing, or destroying the adamantite subject. Disintegrate supplies the other current implementation: it can destroy a legal creature target inside adamantine armor, but the adamantine item receives only one nonstacking Damaged result and cannot become Broken or be destroyed without a more specific explicit override.

The Armor catalogue above already includes the final Grade, DR, Armor HP, Mobility, and weight of each finished exceptional-material armor. The general adjustments remain available for unusual or unique equipment, but players need not calculate percentages during ordinary equipment selection.

Item Quality

Masterwork describes exceptional steel or Tanso-steel construction. It is a material quality rather than a magical enhancement.

Producing such an item requires Skill Mastery in the applicable Craft field. The Mastery permits the attempt; the work still requires appropriate materials, tools, facilities, and time, and an ordinary Craft check resolves uncertainty.

  • A masterwork weapon gains +1 Attack. It gains no separate damage bonus, though Attack Margin may increase.
  • Masterwork armor improves Mobility by 1 toward 0.
  • A masterwork shield improves Mobility by 1 toward 0. Masterwork quality by itself grants no additional Block, Projectile Block, or Reflex bonus.
  • Masterwork quality does not alter Pen, Grade, DR, Armor HP, damage dice, or weight.

Mithril and adamantine items automatically count as masterwork. When the material rules specify an Attack or Mobility result, that result already includes or overrides masterwork quality and does not stack with the ordinary adjustment. Mithril armor therefore improves Mobility by 2 total, while a Mithril shield uses the stated one-point improvement. Adamantine armor and shields receive no Mobility improvement.

Working Mithril or adamantite requires the relevant Craft Skill Mastery in addition to every material-specific requirement. Mastery alone does not grant access to either material.

Use ten times the comparable ordinary item's Typical Value as the normal comparison point for a functional masterwork steel or Tanso-steel item. This is a reference for commissioning exceptional work, not a guaranteed quote or access to a willing craftsperson. A famous maker, political access, unusual commission, elaborate decoration, collector provenance, or court prestige can raise the actual price to one hundred times ordinary value or more. Expense beyond the functional masterwork reference provides no further mechanical benefit unless the item explicitly says otherwise.

Rare materials, exceptional construction, and special facilities can increase the price beyond the functional masterwork benchmark when an item participates in ordinary commerce. If a finished item has an ordinary market value, its entry lists that final Typical Value; do not multiply an already finished masterwork or exceptional-material value again. Nationally controlled exceptional armor and shields have no public Typical Value and cannot be priced from this benchmark.

Energy Armor, Supernatural DR, and Special Construction

Energy Armor and Supernatural DR are grade-less special protections, not materials in the ordinary hierarchy. Their listed DR applies flat without comparing Pen and Grade. Material and other Pen bonuses grant no special bypass unless the protection explicitly says otherwise.

Energy Armor can be carried by a flexible garment while still applying its printed flat DR; damage that exceeds the protection still reaches the wearer. Armor made from supernatural hides, dragon scales, or comparable substances uses the explicit profile created for that item. Exceptional thresholds, immunities, and unusual construction belong to that profile rather than a universal material formula.

Skinning governs recovering monster hides with their exceptional protective properties intact and preserving those properties through crafting. Ordinary collection of scales or other material does not itself retain the creature's supernatural DR. The finished item still uses its own protection profile.

Exceptional elven, dwarven, Mithril, and adamantine armor and shields are nationally controlled and have no public Typical Value. Other unusual constructions use their own entries. Weapon and ammunition weights and values appear in their catalogue rows.

Damaged and Broken Equipment

Equipment does not normally degrade through ordinary use. When an exceptional area effect or a severe material mismatch threatens an item being carried, worn, or wielded, the DM may require the character to make a saving throw, usually Reflex, to protect it. Material, construction, the effect, and the circumstances determine whether a save is required and its DC.

On a failure, the item may become Damaged. The DM chooses the affected function; a -2 or -4 penalty to one or more relevant item statistics is typical. Record separate instances of damage when the DM applies them. There is no fixed number of Damaged results before an item fails. When the DM judges that the accumulated damage or event has made the item nonfunctional, it becomes Broken and cannot be used for its ordinary purpose.

There is no general Sunder action. A deliberate attempt to break an item is resolved by the DM or by the explicit rule that permits it.
