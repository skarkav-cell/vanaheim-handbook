Spells and Spellcasting

1. Magic and Spellcasting at a Glance

Magic is not what it was. In the First Age, words of power could reshape reality and set cities ablaze. Centuries of use, study, and rationalization have worn those workings into smaller, repeatable forms. The spell now called Fireball is one surviving shadow. Modern spells are teachable and dependable—magics sufficiently accepted by the world to function almost mundanely—and their reliability marks how much they have lost.

A spellcaster learns individual spells, spends Magical Energy (ME) to cast them, and uses caster level to determine their power. Vanaheim has no daily preparation of spell slots: a learned spell remains known and can be cast whenever the character meets its requirements and can pay its cost.

The core spellcasting Jobs approach magic through different traditions:

JobTradition and access
PriestDivine magic from a faith-modified Priest list; casting requires High Follower standing.
ShamanSpirit magic from the Shaman list; casting requires the spirit relationship that permits the Job.
PaladinDivine magic from the choosing god's Paladin list; casting requires that god's continuing favor.
HellionDivine magic from the patron Imperial faith's Hellion list; casting requires High Follower standing.
SorcererInnate Sorcery from the Sorcerer list.
WarlockInnate or inherited Elementalism from the Warlock list.
NecromancerRestricted Abyssal Necromancy from the Necromancer list, including preserved legacy spells where Classes permits them.

Each character has one shared ME pool even when several Jobs grant spellcasting. Bard performances are magical talents rather than spells and do not use this chapter's ordinary spellcasting procedure.

Ordinary Necromancer spells use the same casting procedure as other spells, although their source and magical interactions are Necromantic. True Necromancy is the separate freeform practice owned by the Necromancer profile in Classes; it is not an ordinary spell-circle procedure and is not expanded here.

The basic procedure is:

  1. Confirm that the spell is known, currently castable, and within the character's spell-circle limit.
  2. Confirm its target or area, components, ME cost, and required free Concentration.
  3. Use the Main Action, pay ME, and begin casting. Resolve armor Spell Failure when applicable.
  4. Complete the spell after its Spell Speed.
  5. Resolve MR, protective spells, any saving throw, the spell's effect, and applicable protection in the order given under Resolving a Spell.

Combat supplies the detailed initiative, interruption, Defensive Casting, Combat Casting, and damage-routing procedures. Individual spells state their own targets, ranges, areas, durations, components, saving throws, and exceptions.

2. Spellcasters and Spell Circles

Caster Level

Unless a Job, spell, or ability states otherwise, ordinary caster level is:

caster level = half the relevant caster Job level, rounded down

A level-5 Sorcerer therefore has caster level 2, and a level-30 or level-31 caster Job has ordinary caster level 15. A character with more than one caster Job calculates each spell from the Job that grants that spell; caster Job levels are not added together.

Caster level is not ordinarily purchased and is not the same as spell-circle access. Spellcasting Mastery is the explicit Grandmaster exception: add the number of Spellcasting Mastery entries recorded for the casting Job. When one known spell is available through several Jobs, name the casting Job before paying ME and use only that Job's access and caster level. Spellcasting Mastery never grants a spell circle, spell eligibility, or spell knowledge.

Caster level commonly determines damage, duration, range, checks, and other values named by a spell. See Classes — Caster Level for the shared Job calculation. The complete Spellcasting Mastery advancement procedure appears in GM and Beyond.

Spell circles measure the complexity of spells from first through ninth circle. Job level determines the highest circle available through that Job, while the character's Intelligence must also support that circle.

Spell Eligibility and Spell Knowledge

Circle access grants permission to learn appropriate spells; it teaches none automatically. A spell must belong to the character's actual Class eligibility or be granted by another explicit rule.

Public spell lists are intentionally incomplete. A secret spell may belong to one or more Classes without appearing on a public list. Its actual Class eligibility controls who may learn it. Seeing, identifying, or possessing another Class's spell does not by itself grant access.

Moving down one bracket means using the base time for the next lower spell circle. Research never takes less than three days. A ninth-circle spell therefore takes four years at Attribute 19–21, two years at 22–24, and one year at 25 or higher.

Research time may span sessions and established downtime. Record completed research work in the required research book. Once the required research is complete, learning the spell is one advancement purchase at the listed price. The caster then knows it permanently unless an explicit effect says otherwise. The universal session-XP procedure remains DM-facing.

Research Books

Research book is the general rules term for personal working notes. A Mage may call it a spellbook, a Priest may use a prayer book, and a Shaman may keep a written spiritual or ritual record. Its name and appearance do not change the procedure.

Sorcerers, Warlocks, and Necromancers keep their spell research in a research book. Priest, Shaman, Paladin, and Hellion research above fourth circle also requires the appropriate research book.

A research book records incomplete work and may retain the history of earlier research. It is not an instructional manual written for strangers. Another eligible caster who successfully deciphers it may discover that a spell exists and gain direction for beginning ordinary research, but possession of the book does not teach the completed spell.

The book must be available while the character performs and records the research and when the completed spell is learned. Once a spell is completed, the book is no longer required to cast it and losing the book does not erase the spell.

If a research book is lost or destroyed before the spell is completed, its unfinished research record is lost and that work must be rebuilt. A grimoire or another explicit rule may state a different result.

Grimoires

A grimoire is an intentionally instructional magical item rather than another caster's personal notes. Each grimoire states what it teaches, who can learn from it, and what XP or other requirements remain. It may teach a spell for no XP or for less than the ordinary cost.

A grimoire spell need not appear on a public list, but it must establish the Classes eligible to learn it. Some grimoires teach several Classes. Rumors speak of rarer works containing spells that anyone can learn.

New Spells

A Master-level caster may invent a genuinely new spell belonging to that caster's Class. Spell design, approval, time, and XP are advanced magic and DM-facing work outside this core procedure.

4. Casting a Spell

Requirements and Payment

Ordinary spellcasting uses the Main Action. Before beginning, confirm all of the following:

  • the spell is known and the caster currently meets its Class, circle, Intelligence, and other access requirements;
  • the target or area is legal and within range;
  • every required component is available;
  • enough Concentration capacity is free; and
  • the caster can pay the full ME cost.

Declare the spell and its target or area, then pay the full ME cost when casting begins. The spell remains in progress until its Spell Speed reaches completion. If the target dies or otherwise becomes invalid before completion, the spell is wasted and its ME remains spent.

A caster who cannot pay the full ME cost cannot ordinarily begin. As a last resort, the DM may permit voluntary spending into negative ME and may require a Will save. Each complete 2 ME below zero causes 1 LP damage: -1 causes none, -2 causes 1 LP, and -4 causes another 1 LP. The DM may also impose permanent damage to maximum ME. Ordinary ME-draining effects stop at 0 unless they explicitly say otherwise.

Spell Speed

Spell Speed delays completion after casting begins. Subtract the spell's Speed from the caster's current initiative count. A Speed 3 spell begun on initiative 25 completes at the end of initiative 22, after ordinary actions at 22. Numeric completion continues below 0 within the same round.

The full timing procedure, including simultaneous completions, Readied spells, Round 0, and interruption during the casting interval, appears under Combat — Spell Speed and Scheduled Completion and Combat — Spell Interruption.

Components

  • V — Verbal: the caster must be able to speak.
  • S — Somatic: the caster must be able to perform the spell's gestures.
  • M — Material: the caster must possess and use the listed material, commonly from a component satchel. It is consumed only when the component or spell says so.

An individual spell may omit one or more components or state a specific exception. A silenced caster cannot supply a required verbal component, and a caster unable to gesture cannot supply a required somatic component without an explicit permission.

Armor Spell Failure

Worn armor's Spell Failure applies to somatic casting when the caster's Job, faith, talent, or another explicit source does not permit that spell in the armor. Roll percentile dice when casting begins. A result within the armor's percentage loses the spell; its full ME cost remains spent.

A spell with no somatic component does not make this armor Spell Failure roll. A waiver applies only to the spells and armor named by its source. Priest, Paladin, Hellion, and Warlock permissions therefore do not automatically waive Spell Failure for spells learned through another Job.

Concentration Capacity

Concentration capacity equals the caster's Intelligence modifier. A spell writes its requirement as initial/maintenance:

  • The first number is the free capacity required to begin casting.
  • The second is the capacity that remains occupied while the spell is maintained.
  • Do not add the values. Capacity meeting the initial requirement may immediately become the maintained capacity.
  • 1/-, a bare 1, or 1/ requires 1 free capacity to cast and occupies none afterward.

A caster may release one or more maintained spells as a Free Action during that caster's own turn, never off-turn as a reaction. Unconsciousness, death, or Stunned ends all maintained spells. Paralysis alone does not. If capacity falls below the amount occupied, enough maintained spells end to fit the new capacity.

Actual damage can also break maintained Concentration. Without Combat Casting, one result that leaves at least 1 actual damage ends every maintained spell. A character who knows Combat Casting instead rolls d20 equal to or below half Wisdom; success preserves the maintained spells and failure ends them. Damage reduced to 0 requires no roll. The complete combat handling appears under Combat — Damage and Maintained Spells.

Touch Delivery

An instantaneous touch spell must be delivered as part of its casting. A willing target normally requires no Attack. Against an unwilling target, use:

1d20 + BAB + Dexterity modifier + 5

This is a melee or ranged touch Attack as the spell requires. Dodge prevents delivery. Parry or Block normally creates enough contact for the touch effect to resolve unless the spell says that deflecting its hand, implement, or projectile defeats it.

The touch Attack supplies contact rather than weapon damage. The spell supplies its damage, saving throw, MR, condition, and other effects. If an instantaneous touch Attack misses, the spell ends without effect.

A touch effect remains charged only when its own entry grants a duration or charging procedure. Such an entry may place the charge on a hand, another unarmed limb, or a weapon and states when it discharges. There is no general held-touch-charge rule.

Weapon-Carried Spell Charges

A spellcaster may place a spell into a held melee weapon only when that spell's own entry explicitly permits weapon delivery. A successful weapon attack deals its ordinary weapon result and then delivers the spell. The spell entry states how long the charge lasts, whether a miss retains it, what causes discharge, which magical defenses apply, and when the charge ends. No other touch spell gains weapon delivery by inference.

A melee weapon may carry no more than one weapon-carried spell charge at a time, regardless of the charge's source. A weapon that already carries a charge is not an eligible target for another until the first charge discharges or ends. A spell-specific entry may define its discharge and duration, but does not increase this limit unless it expressly says so.

5. Resolving a Spell

Resolution Order

When several defenses apply to the same incoming hostile spell or magical effect, use this order:

  1. Magic Resistance: successful MR completely resists the magic for that creature.
  2. Preventive spells: resolve ongoing effects that absorb, turn, reflect, deflect, redirect, or otherwise stop the incoming spell.
  3. Saving throw: roll the save and apply the result stated by the spell.
  4. Damage protection: apply Magic Protection, other applicable DR, and ongoing spells that reduce damage.
  5. Physical Energy mitigation: apply one eligible PE option.
  6. Damage pools: route what remains through Temporary HP, HP, and LP.

When several ongoing beneficial spells respond within the same stage, resolve the highest-circle spell first and continue downward. A spell may state a more specific interaction or precedence.

Spell Difficulty Class and Saves

Unless a spell supplies another complete formula, its save DC is:

10 + caster level + Charisma modifier + ART

The spell identifies Fortitude, Reflex, or Will and states what success does. There is no universal successful-save result: success may negate, halve, reduce, delay, or otherwise change the spell. Meeting or exceeding the DC succeeds, and natural 1 or 20 has no automatic result beyond its final total.

When a successful save halves damage, halve the spell's raw damage and round down before applying MP or another applicable protection.

Magic Resistance and Magic Protection

Magic Resistance (MR) is a percentile chance to resist magic completely. Roll it only when the spell says MR applies. A successful MR result prevents that spell from affecting that creature, leaving nothing for its saving throw or MP to resolve unless the spell states an exception.

Magic Protection (MP) is passive flat reduction against applicable spell damage. It is not ME and cannot be spent. Apply MP only after MR fails and any saving throw has changed the damage.

MR, a saving throw, and MP are separate defenses. Do not add one to another.

Spell Damage

After establishing the complete damage-dice expression for one result, roll no more than four damage dice. Replace every die beyond the fourth with fixed +2 inside the damage-dice subtotal:

  • 6d8 becomes 4d8 + 4.
  • 10d6 becomes 4d6 + 12.

The converted values remain part of the dice subtotal for any explicit multiplication. This conversion applies only to damage; it does not limit healing, recovery, PE mitigation, checks, or another non-damage roll.

Calculate raw damage first. Apply a save that modifies that raw damage, then applicable MP and other protection, then one eligible PE mitigation option, and finally route the remaining result through the target's damage pools. Combat — Damage Resolution owns the complete common procedure.

Effect-Owned Exceptions

Every spell entry defines its targets, area, duration, components, Save, MR, Concentration, and effect. A spell-specific rule overrides this shared procedure only within its stated scope. Resolve the spell using its stated rules together with these shared procedures.

6. Spellcasting in Combat

Ordinary spellcasting uses the Main Action and cannot be combined with a Full Attack. The caster declares the spell and its target or area, pays its ME, and schedules completion through Spell Speed. A spell remains vulnerable during that casting interval.

Beginning an ordinary spell while threatened provokes an attack of opportunity. Combat Casting supplies the established Defensive Casting attempt, the combined casting-and-melee sequence, and its protection against interruption. It does not create attack passes or grant another spell.

A hit that leaves at least 1 actual damage or a successful Grab can ruin a spell during its casting interval. Actual damage taken after completion cannot retroactively ruin the spell, although it may end spells maintained through Concentration. Damage reduced to 0 triggers neither result.

Combat owns the complete procedures and edge cases:

  • Spell Speed and scheduled completion
  • Delay, Ready, and pre-combat preparation
  • Interruption and failed spells
  • Defensive Casting
  • Combat Casting
  • Damage and maintained spells
  • Casting while grappling

7. Counterspelling

Ordinary Counterspelling

Ordinary counterspelling requires preparation and the same spell:

  1. Ready an action to counter a spell before the hostile casting begins.
  2. When the trigger occurs, the countering caster must see the hostile caster and perceive at least one remaining verbal or somatic component.
  3. Make a trained Knowledge (Spellcraft) check to identify the spell, applying the modifiers below.
  4. On success, if the countering caster can cast that same spell, spend its normal ME and consume the readied action to cast it as an immediate interrupt. Ignore the countering spell's ordinary Spell Speed. The hostile spell is countered without another check.

Apply another -4 when only the somatic component can be perceived, and -4 when only the verbal component can be perceived. These penalties combine with the circle penalty.

Quickened spells cannot be countered through this ordinary procedure. Neither can a spell-like ability or a spell with no perceptible verbal or somatic component.

After a failed identification check, a caster who has Dispel Magic available may still use it through the readied action. The Dispel Magic spell owns the dispel check and its limitations. The secret grimoire spell named Counterspell is also separate; its own entry will govern its ME-matching and negation rules.

Knowledge (Spellcraft)

Knowledge (Spellcraft) is trained only. It cannot be attempted untrained at half Intelligence. In addition to counterspelling, it has these current uses:

  • Identify casting: recognize a spell while it is cast, using the counterspelling modifiers above. Identification does not grant the spell or permission to cast it.
  • Decipher research: understand another caster's research book. Apply -4 for every spell circle by which the recorded spell exceeds the highest circle the reader can currently cast. Success reveals the research and the spell's apparent purpose; ordinary eligibility, XP, time, and grimoire rules still apply.
  • Analyze magical effects: examine an active or recorded magical structure or interpret auras revealed by Detect Magic. Concealment, damage, complexity, or incomplete access may modify the check.

Skills owns the ordinary check procedure and the Knowledge (Spellcraft) field. Individual spells own exceptional detection behavior.

8. Magical Devices

Activating a Device

A character must possess the corresponding Scrolls, Wands, or Staves Talent to activate that device type.

If the stored spell belongs to any of the user's actual Class spell eligibilities, activation requires no check, even when the spell's circle is higher than the user can currently cast. Secret or unprinted Class eligibility counts. If the spell does not belong to the user's Class eligibility, make an Intelligence check. Success activates the item; failure consumes the scroll or one charge and produces no spell effect.

Activation normally uses a Main Action. The stored spell resolves immediately without the user's ordinary Spell Speed, and the user spends no personal ME. The device supplies its caster level, numerical effect, save DC, ART adjustments, and other stored values. One scroll or one charge supplies the activation unless the item says otherwise.

The stored spell retains its legal range and target requirements. A touch-range Wand therefore still requires the appropriate touch Attack. An individual item may define a different action, activation time, or other bounded exception.

Scrolls

A Scroll may hold a spell above the user's current casting circle. Class eligibility, rather than current circle, determines whether activation is automatic.

The Scroll is consumed when activated. A failed off-list activation attempt also consumes it. Its magical parchment disintegrates completely.

Wands

A Wand applies its spell to one legal target. A self-only spell can affect only the user, a touch spell requires contact, and a ranged spell functions to its stored range. A Wand does not turn its spell into an area effect.

A Wand remains intact while at least one charge remains. Spending its final charge makes it crack and collapse harmlessly into unusable pieces.

Staves

Unless its own entry says otherwise, one Staff charge applies its spell to every eligible creature within 60 feet. A beneficial spell affects the wielder's recognized allies; a hostile spell affects every other eligible creature in range.

A Staff recognizes established companions. An unfamiliar friendly creature is safely included when willingly touching the wielder or one of those companions at activation. Proximity alone gives no guaranteed protection, although the DM may accept clear circumstances. Hostile contact does not fool the Staff.

A Staff remains intact at zero charges. An individual Staff may replace the ordinary range, targeting, or affected-creature rule. A Staff containing an area spell must define its own placement and overlap behavior.

Recharging Wands and Staves

Recharge is a spell shared by several spellcasting Classes. A caster can use a Class's version of Recharge only when the caster knows both Recharge and the exact spell stored in the device through that Class. Device access, Class eligibility without knowledge, and possession of a Scroll do not replace either known spell.

A spell may be available to Recharge through several Classes. A device may instead be impossible for any ordinary caster to Recharge when no Class can supply both required spells. The Recharge spell entry owns the complete procedure, costs, time, and number of charges restored. An individual device may state an exception.

9. Metamagic

Metamagic techniques modify ordinary spells personally cast through Sorcerer, Warlock, or Necromancer. The shared Mage progression in Classes grants one Metamagic selection at Mage Job level 10 and another at levels 14, 18, 22, 26, and 30.

Declare Metamagic when casting begins. Add its extra ME cost to the spell's base ME cost and pay the complete amount at once. If two Metamagic abilities modify the same spell, add both costs. A failed or interrupted spell refunds none of that ME.

A spell can receive no more than two different Metamagic abilities. The same ability cannot be applied twice. Metamagic cannot modify a Scroll, Wand, Staff, Ritual Spell, Bard performance, Talent, spell-like ability, or True Necromancy unless an explicit rule says otherwise.

Metamagic Catalogue

Persistent Spell — Extra ME 3. A spell maintained through Concentration continues for 1d4 rounds after that maintenance ends, including voluntary release, lost Concentration, unconsciousness, or the caster's death. The spell no longer occupies Concentration during those rounds. Dispel Magic, successful Magic Resistance, or another effect that destroys or negates the spell ends or prevents it normally; persistence does not protect it. Persistent Spell does not make an instantaneous effect repeat.

Quicken Spell — Extra ME 6. Cast the spell as a Free Action with Spell Speed 0; it resolves immediately. Only one Quickened spell may be cast per round. Its ordinary components, ME, targeting, Spell Failure, and other requirements still apply. A Quickened spell cannot be countered through ordinary counterspelling.

Empower Spell — Extra ME 3 per +1 DC. Increase the spell's save DC by +1 to +5, chosen when casting begins. The maximum +5 increase costs 15 extra ME.

Silent Spell — Extra ME 2. Ignore the spell's verbal component. Other components remain unchanged.

Still Spell — Extra ME 2. Ignore the spell's somatic component. Because the modified spell has no somatic component, worn armor does not make its ordinary somatic Spell Failure roll. Other components remain unchanged.

Shape Spell — Extra ME 2. Sculpt safe spaces inside the spell's existing area, allowing chosen creatures or portions of the area to remain unaffected. Shape Spell does not increase the spell's range, total area, damage, or number of targets and cannot modify a spell that has no area to shape.

Penetrating Spell — Extra ME 4. Halve the Magic Protection applied against this spell and round down.

High Density Spell — Extra ME 4. Reduce each target's Magic Resistance against this casting by caster level + 10 percentage points, to a minimum of 0%.

Manaburn Spell — Extra ME 4. Apply only to a spell with one target. In addition to the spell's ordinary resolution, the target makes a separate Will save against the spell's normal DC. Failure removes ME equal to the underlying spell's base ME cost; success removes half that amount, rounded down. Manaburn cannot reduce ME below 0. Its drain excludes every Metamagic surcharge.

Manawall Spell — Extra ME 1–10. Choose the extra ME when casting begins. Until that spell completes or fails, Manawall absorbs up to twice the chosen amount in damage that would otherwise reach the caster. The selected ME is spent even if no damage arrives. Damage fully absorbed by Manawall does not interrupt the spell.

Repeating Spell — Extra ME equal to 2 + the spell's base ME cost. After the original spell completes successfully, it resolves again at the same initiative on the following round without another action or ME payment. Use the original target or area, but make new attacks, saves, damage rolls, and other variable rolls. If the first casting is interrupted or fails, nothing repeats. The repetition cannot affect a target that no longer exists or is otherwise no longer eligible for that spell.

Extend Spell — Extra ME 3. Increase the spell's range by 60 feet and double its stated duration. Extend Spell cannot modify a touch-range spell. It does not create a fixed duration for an instantaneous spell or for a spell that lasts only while Concentration is maintained.

10. Ritual Spells

Ritual Spells are large or specialized acts of Magic that operate outside ordinary spellcasting. They commonly use magic circles, extended preparation, special materials or locations, multiple participants, stored or environmental power, or other requirements defined by the ritual. Rituals can establish magical workshops, link prepared teleportation sites, create magical fortifications, or accomplish other workings whose scale or structure does not fit an ordinary spell. These are examples of what rituals may accomplish, not general abilities granted by Ritual Spell eligibility.

Every Mage Job makes the character eligible to learn Ritual Spells, but grants no ritual automatically. A Mage can learn or perform only a ritual whose circle does not exceed the highest spell circle that Mage can currently cast through the qualifying Mage Job. An individual ritual may explicitly permit Priests or other non-Mage participants; that exception grants access only to that ritual. A ritual may be performed by one caster or may require or benefit from several participants; the individual ritual decides.

Spiritual Wrath is an example intended for Priests and Paladins. It is not an ordinary spell. This entry grants no ability to perform it; its complete requirements and procedure must first be supplied by the DM.

The DM determines which rituals exist, where they can be discovered, and whether a character can learn one. Every ritual supplies its own casting time, participants, ME or other power source, materials, site, magic-circle requirements, checks, risks, interruption rules, and result. Ritual Spell eligibility grants no default procedure for improvising a ritual. Metamagic and Clarity do not modify Ritual Spells unless an explicit rule says otherwise.

Two known priestly examples are Land of Stability, which can protect prepared territory from natural disasters, and The Great Circle, a cooperative working used to cleanse and consecrate ground for a temple or sanctuary. These descriptions grant no access or casting procedure. A character needs the ritual's complete requirements and procedure from the DM.

11. Reading a Spell Entry

Read each spell from top to bottom: its header, an Effect section that first explains the magic and then gives its rules, and any longer Description that follows. The opening tells you what the spell does in the world before you resolve its mechanics. All required rules remain together in the Effect section.

Job Abbreviations

Compact rules blocks may abbreviate Jobs as follows:

PRI Priest · SHA Shaman · PAL Paladin · HEL Hellion · SOR Sorcerer · WAR Warlock · NEC Necromancer

These abbreviations identify Jobs in spell entries and compact rules blocks.

Standard Header

Each ordinary spell uses the following header fields. Values such as None, No, Self, and Instantaneous state an explicit answer.

  • Class/Circle: the Job-and-circle pairs for the spell, with a separate pair for each Job: SOR 3 · PRI 3 · NEC 3.
  • School: the spell's magical school.
  • ME: the Magical Energy paid to cast it.
  • Spell Speed: its initiative delay or longer casting time.
  • Components: its required V, S, and M components, or None.
  • Range: how far from the caster the spell can reach.
  • Target or Area: the creature, object, or area the spell affects. An entry may specify both a target and an area.
  • Duration: how long the effect remains.
  • Concentration: its initial/maintenance requirement.
  • Save: the saving throw and result, such as Will negates, Reflex half, or None.
  • Magic Resistance: Yes when ordinary Magic Resistance applies and No when it does not.

A specific, priced, unusual, or consumed material component receives a Material line directly beneath the header. Mechanically meaningful descriptors or unusual delivery restrictions may likewise appear there when useful.

Brewable identifies a spell that may be made into a potion. Cannot Be Scribed marks an exception to the ordinary Scroll rule.

Mechanical Effect

The Effect section explains what the spell does and gives its complete rules: the exact roll or DC, damage or healing, damage type, Penetration, scaling, saving-throw result, and any operational exception. The rules needed to resolve the spell are together in this section; the longer Description adds atmosphere and lore.

Description

An optional Description expands on the spell's appearance, sensation, history, customary interpretation, old accounts, material symbolism, or uncertain and cryptic observations. It adds atmosphere and lore alongside the complete rules in Effect.

Descriptive uncertainty does not secretly alter the reliable game procedure above it. It may imply that scholars understand only part of the working, that older forms were greater, or that unusual magic exists beyond the catalogue, but any additional mechanical effect requires an explicit rule.
